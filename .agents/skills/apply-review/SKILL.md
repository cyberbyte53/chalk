# Apply a review

A **review** is one sitting of the person's, handed over in one line: `apply review 20261026-1734 (4 threads)`. It lists every thread they started or replied to since their last handover: new comments, and old ones they reopened. Read it with the `comments` tool (`review: "<id>"`; `"latest"` when the line names none). Each thread has its board, its target (an address: `piece/part`), the piece's source line, what the piece showed (`snapshot`), the commit it was made on, what the person said, and every message since. Format and rules: `platform/docs/comments.md`.

You read and write threads only with the tools: `comments` to read, `comment` to write (the `chalk` server writes as a fixer). Never edit the comments.yaml files or authoring/reviews/. The writer refuses what a fixer may not do (close, reopen, withdraw, delete) and says why: tell the person rather than working around it.

A thread on a prop (`library/props/…`) is about that prop wherever it is used, not one board: a change to its fields, actions or signals is its definition and a thread first (the build-props skill, § 2), then code, checking every place `affected` lists. A thread on a planning file is fixed in that file; what it reaches is said by the pipeline step (files to be checked again).

## Steps

1. **Read every thread** in the review. For each, open `source` at its line and `show` its address (its board, or its piece: `content/…/<board>#<target>`). See what the person saw. For a reopened thread, read its messages: what you did last time and why they reopened it. Read them together first: several threads often share one cause ("he wants figures here").
2. **One plan for the whole review, in chat** (Cursor's plan mode when there): first the changes that reach beyond one board (a prop to change across lessons, a kit or pattern function, a rule for the chapter), each with its reason and what `affected` says it reaches (ask it with exactly what you plan to touch: the file, the lines, or the declaration, `file#name`; quote the counts and the chapters); then one line per thread. Ask questions here, not on the threads. Wait for the person's go; for a few local fixes, or when the person said "just do it", go straight on.
3. **Before changing anything**, write each thread's line of the plan on it: `comment` with `status: "planned"`. The person sees it under the board.
4. **Apply.** Make the edits. For each thread, `show` its board or piece and check the change answers it (with `steps` if it's about what happens). Then `affected` with `diff: true`: if it reaches more than the plan said, say so and `show` what it adds (a whole chapter: `show` the chapter, which checks every board).
5. **Review it fresh, always.** Have an agent that didn't make the changes run `self-review` on the boards touched, and what `affected` lists, however small the fixes. The Chalk tree shows a lesson changed since its last review as **to self-review**; don't hand back while it does. It writes on your threads where the fix isn't right yet (answer each with a message or a new plan; a thread can't go ready until you have), and files regressions against the thread whose change broke something elsewhere (fix those first: a thread can't be ready while one is open).
6. **Answer every thread**: `comment` with `status: "ready"` and what changed, **before → after**, so the person judges from the thread without opening the diff: for a board or a prop, what it showed and what it shows now (the `show` lines, and say a picture is one `show` away); for a planning file, the old words and the new. The files changed and what they reach are recorded. No status when you didn't change anything (a question, a reason not to). Never close one: only the person closes.
   A thread whose place is gone (`pnpm check`'s threads step lists it: a board renamed, a piece merged, words rewritten) is **re-pointed** (`comment` with its `id` and its new `at`, saying where it went and why) or **answered** (ready, saying what happened to it). Never leave one pointing at nothing.
7. **Feedback in chat** about a board ("the slider's still too small"): record it with `comment` (`at`: the board or piece's address, `from: "chat"`, and `plan` once they've agreed to one: the thread starts planned) so it lives on the board like any thread, filed in the person's name, then handle it the same way.
8. **Hand back, and wait.** Only after step 5's review is in. Say it's ready, what to look at, and the reviewer's verdict. The person checks the boards in the preview and the diff, and replies on a thread (it reopens, and goes in their next review) or closes it. **Never commit until the person says they're happy.** Then run `pnpm check` and commit with the message `review <id>: <one line>`, with the review file.
9. **Patterns:** if the same kind of thread could come back elsewhere, say so and suggest the `wrap-up` step (a check, an example, or a guide line).

Without a review ("handle the comments on Light"), do the same with `comments` filtered by the chapter's board prefix and `unanswered: true`.
