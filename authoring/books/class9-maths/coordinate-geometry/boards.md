# Boards: Coordinate Geometry

Each board and question family of `plan.md`'s lessons: what it is for, its beat in the story, what it
needs (in words, never a prop's name), the objectives and outline ids it serves (its heading), and the
props it uses. Its state (built, reviewed) is worked out from the build, never written here.

## two-numbers-for-a-place

Order: discover → state. The lesson starts from what the student knows, a number line (the bus board, a refresher on its own): one number fixes a point on it. Then a second number line rises from that point, and the plane is built from number lines standing on one: a pair is how far along the first line, then how far up the line that rises there. The need for the second number comes out of the story (one number brings Riya to a street with five houses on it), and "two numbers, in order" is named at the end, not announced at the start. The order of the two numbers is taught once, where the source teaches it (the seating plan).

### bus-stops  (o1; s3.1-number-line)
Purpose: a refresher on what they know, the number line, as its own short story. A bus on a straight road starts at the bus stand, 0; stops are numbered 1, 2, 3, … one way and −1, −2, −3, … the other. The student moves the bus stop by stop and sees that one number fixes where it is on the line: its size says how far from the stand, its sign which way. Not Riya's story: one sentence at most leads on to the town ("on a line, one number was enough").
Beat: none: a refresher before Riya's story starts.
Needs: one straight line, 0 marked as the bus stand, numbers both ways, stops along it; the bus moved one stop per tap of ← or →, from where it is; "go to 3", "go to −2", and the bus at a stop with "how far from the stand, and which side?".
Props: coordinatePlane (core on one line; places: the stops; walker: the bus)

### street-and-house  (o1, o2; s3.1, s3.1-number-line, s3.1-I, fig3.1)
Purpose: the main road is a number line, and "Street 4" walks Riya to 4 on it: on a line, one number is enough. Then Street 4 rises from the 4, a second number line going up, with five houses on it: which is Aman's? A beat later he replies "House 5", a number on the line that rose, and she walks up to it. Then every street rises, and the town is there. Any house can be made Aman's; Riya goes back to the corner, and the class says how she gets there before walking. Departs from Fig. 3.1: every street runs north of the main road, so the walk is always along, then up; and the town has six streets, not eight, so each block is big enough to tap on a phone. H (Street 2, House 5) keeps its numbers.
Beat: Riya walks to Aman's house, where the two plan their stall: "Street 4" is not enough, "House 5" is.
Needs: the main road drawn as a number line from the corner (Riya there), with no streets yet; a street rising as a number line from one mark, its houses appearing on it; then every street rising. Riya moves one block per tap of ↑ ↓ ← →, on roads only, and her address shows as she moves. On Street 4 its five houses light, and Aman's house is marked only once "House 5" is said. Tapping any house makes it Aman's and sends Riya back to the corner.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; places: houses; walker)

### dot-on-paper  (o1; s3.1-II, fig3.2, ex3.1, ex3.1-q1)
Purpose: a dot on a notebook page, which has no roads: how do you say exactly where it is? The book's own ways, one at a time, each shading where the dot could still be: "in the upper half" leaves half the page, "near the left edge" as well still a patch; "5 cm from the left edge" a whole line, drawn the full height of the page; only "9 cm above the bottom" as well leaves one point, the dot, where the two lines cross. Two distances from two fixed lines, the left edge and the bottom, fix it (Fig. 3.2). Then the dot is the student's: dragged anywhere, its two distances follow it. No story, and small numbers: the page is squared in centimetres, so the student counts them.
Beat: none: a notebook page, outside the story.
Needs: a notebook page squared in centimetres, with no numbers on its edges at first (an edge is numbered once a distance along it is given), and the dot on it; the four descriptions one at a time, each shading where the dot could still be, each distance drawn as a full-length line and measured from its edge; once both are given, the dot becomes one the student drags, its two distances from the edges drawn and written as it moves.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; clues; reading a point: its two distances)

### seating-plan  (o2; activity1, fig3.3)
Purpose: a desk is given by its column and row, column first, so (5, 3) and (3, 5) are two different desks. This is where the lesson teaches the order of the two numbers.
Beat: none: the classroom, where the class plans the fair.
Needs: the classroom as desks in 10 columns and 7 rows, columns numbered from the left and rows from the front, both named; tap a desk to see its pair; (5, 3) and (3, 5) both stay marked once tapped. Sonia's desk is (4, 1), labelled.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; places: desks)

### two-lines  (o3; s3.1-idea, box-descartes, fig3.4, s3.4, summary)
Purpose: each time, two lines at right angles did it: one across (the main road, the bottom of the page, the front row) and one up (the street, the left edge, a column). The first number says how far across, the second how far up. That idea, Descartes', is coordinate geometry. The lesson ends with summary point 1.
Beat: the house, the dot and the desk seen again, side by side.
Needs: the house, the dot and the desk again, each with its route along, then up, drawn in colour so its two lines stand out, and its two numbers; Descartes' story in words (no portrait).
Props: coordinatePlane, placed still; Descartes in words

### questions/bus-to-stop  (o1; s3.1-number-line)

## the-cartesian-plane

Order: discover → state for why the lines need negative numbers and a zero; state → see it hold for the names (axes, origin, directions, quadrants).

### number-line  (o4; s3.2, s3.2-number-line, fig3.5)
Purpose: recap: on a number line, distance r from the origin on the positive side is r, on the negative side −r.
Beat: none: a recap before the town grows.
Needs: a number line from −5 to 5 with the origin and one unit marked; tap a point to read its number.
Props: coordinatePlane (core)

### town-grows  (o4, o5; def-directions)
Purpose: the town grows past Riya's corner: new streets to the west of where she started, and houses south of the main road. Counting from her corner, those go the other way, so they get negative numbers, and her corner is 0 on both roads. Not in the source; it is why the axes are number lines, with a zero and both signs.
Beat: the town grows past the corner Riya started from: new streets west, houses south.
Needs: lesson 1's town, whose map extends west and south of Riya's corner; new houses appear there; numbering them from the corner gives −1, −2, … west along the road and south down a street; tap one to see its address, both numbers signed; Riya walks to one, going west or south as a sign says.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; places; walker)

### two-number-lines  (o4; s3.2-perpendicular, fig3.7, def-axes, def-origin)
Purpose: as the town's two roads at right angles were number lines, any point in a plane is located with two number lines at right angles, one horizontal and one vertical, crossing at their zeroes: the x-axis, the y-axis and the origin O. They are the town's main road and Riya's street, and O is the corner she started from. (That the pair could face any way, and why one pair is agreed on, is shown once coordinates are defined: any-axes.)
Beat: the town's main road and Riya's street become the axes; her corner is O.
Needs: two number lines, one horizontal (X′X) and one vertical (Y′Y), brought together until their zeroes meet; the crossing named O; the town map fading in behind and out again.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; backdrop)

### axes-and-directions  (o4; def-directions, fig3.8)
Purpose: OX and OY are the positive directions of the axes, OX′ and OY′ the negative ones.
Beat: none: the axes alone.
Needs: the axes; tap each half of each axis to see its name and sign.
Props: coordinatePlane (core)

### quadrants  (o5; def-quadrants, def-cartesian-plane, fig3.9, ex3.2, ex3.2-q1, s3.4, summary)
Purpose: the axes divide the plane into four quadrants, numbered I to IV anticlockwise from OX: the town's four parts. The plane is the Cartesian, coordinate or xy-plane, and the axes are the coordinate axes. The lesson ends with summary points 2 to 5.
Beat: the town's four parts are the four quadrants, Aman's house in I and his aunt's in III.
Needs: the plane; a sweep from OX anticlockwise numbers the quadrants I to IV; tap a part to see its name; the town's four parts shaded to match, the corner at O.
Props: coordinatePlane (core; backdrop; tintsQuadrants)

### questions/name-the-lines  (o4, o5; ex3.2, ex3.2-q1)
Purpose: Ex 3.2 Q1 in the book's words: the names of the axes, the quadrants and the origin.
Beat: none.
Needs: each part of the question alone; the names as options, worded alike.
Props: coordinatePlane (core), drawn still

### questions/tap-the-part  (o4, o5; def-directions, def-quadrants, ex3.2-q1)
Purpose: each half of each axis and each quadrant, found on the plane by its name.
Beat: none.
Needs: the axes and quadrants, each part tappable; a name asked for; a wrong tap's note names the slip.
Props: coordinatePlane (core)

## coordinates-of-a-point

Order: state → see it hold for the abscissa and ordinate (a definition); discover → state for the signs in each quadrant (found by moving a point through them).

### distances-from-axes  (o6; s3.2-distances, fig3.10)
Purpose: a point's distance from the y-axis is measured along the x-axis, and its distance from the x-axis along the y-axis. P is 4 and 3 units away, along the positive directions; Q is 6 and 2 units away, along the negative ones. That is Riya's walk: along, then up or down.
Beat: Riya walks from O to P and to Q: along, then up or down.
Needs: graph-paper axes with P and Q; the perpendiculars from each point to both axes, with their lengths and the direction each is measured in; Riya walks from O to P, and to Q, along the same lines.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point; walker)

### abscissa-and-ordinate  (o6; def-abscissa, def-ordinate)
Purpose: the x-coordinate (abscissa) is the distance from the y-axis with a sign, + along OX and − along OX′; the y-coordinate (ordinate) likewise. P has +4 and +3, Q has −6 and −2.
Beat: none: the definition, on graph paper.
Needs: a point the student drags on graph paper, with its perpendiculars to the axes and its signed distances named abscissa and ordinate; the signs change as the point crosses an axis.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point)

### writing-coordinates  (o7, o10; def-coordinates, note-unique, remark-convention, epigraph)
Purpose: the coordinates are written x first, in brackets: P is (4, 3) and Q is (−6, −2). (3, 4) is not (4, 3). x first is not a fact but an agreement the whole world keeps, so a message means the same thing to everyone, as Aman's address did. Riya writes down where the first stalls stand.
Beat: Riya writes down where the first stalls stand on the fair ground's plan.
Needs: tap anywhere and a point appears with its coordinates; the swapped pair shown as a different point; the fair ground's plan with two stalls and their coordinates.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point; places: the fair ground's stalls)

### reading-points  (o7; example1, fig3.11)
Purpose: worked example: read the abscissa, ordinate and coordinates of B, M, L and S.
Beat: none: the book's worked example.
Needs: the figure's four points with dashed perpendiculars to both axes, read one at a time.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point)

### points-on-axes  (o8; example2, fig3.12, rule-on-axes, rule-origin)
Purpose: a point on the x-axis is at zero distance from it, so it is (x, 0); a point on the y-axis is (0, y); the origin is (0, 0). In the town: a house on the main road needs no walk up, so its second number is 0. Worked example: A, B, C, D and E on the axes, E at (2/3, 0).
Beat: a house on the main road needs no walk up, so its second number is 0.
Needs: a point dragged onto an axis, one coordinate becoming 0, and onto O for (0, 0); "every point with y = 0" lights the whole x-axis; the figure's five points with each one's "why".
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point; clues: "every point with y = 0")

### signs-and-quadrants  (o9; rule-signs, fig3.13)
Purpose: every point in quadrant I is (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −) and IV (+, −), because each quadrant is bounded by those halves of the axes.
Beat: none: a point moved through the quadrants, then a guessing round.
Needs: a point dragged through all four quadrants, the signs of its coordinates shown; each quadrant's pattern filled in once the point has been there. Then a guessing round: a point appears, the class calls its quadrant from its signs before it is shown.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point, each quadrant's sign pattern)

### any-axes  (o7; s3.2-perpendicular, fig3.6, remark-convention)
Purpose: the axes are a choice we make. Move O anywhere, turn the pair any way: a point stays where it is, and only its coordinates change. So everyone uses the same pair, the agreed x- and y-axes, just as everyone writes x first. Not in the source as a picture; it is what "they can point any way" and "only a convention" mean.
Beat: why everyone uses the same pair of axes, as everyone writes x first.
Needs: a fixed point P with its perpendiculars to both axes and its coordinates, signed, to two decimal places; the student drags O to move the axes and turns them about O with a handle; P's coordinates change as they do and P doesn't move; put back, its coordinates are what they were.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: shift and turn)

### find-the-point  (o7; ex3.2-q2, fig3.14, s3.4, summary)
Purpose: read coordinates from a figure, and find the point with given coordinates, including points on the axes (L, M). The lesson ends with summary points 6 to 10.
Beat: none: the book's figure; the lesson's summary.
Needs: the figure's eight points with dashed lines to the axes; read a point's coordinates, and name the point at given coordinates.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point)

### questions/read-a-point  (o6, o7; def-coordinates, note-unique)
Purpose: read a marked point's coordinates; the wrong options are the slips: y written first, one sign read the wrong way.
Beat: none.
Needs: a point on the axes with its dashed lines to them; four pairs to choose from.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point)

### questions/fig-3-14  (o7, o8; ex3.2-q2, fig3.14)
Purpose: Ex 3.2 Q2: Fig. 3.14 read both ways, a point's coordinates and the point at a pair, L and M on an axis.
Beat: none: the book's figure.
Needs: the figure's eight points with dashed lines to the axes; a point or a pair asked for.
Props: coordinatePlane (core: reading a point)

### questions/quadrant-or-axis  (o8, o9; ex3.3, ex3.3-q1)
Purpose: Ex 3.3 Q1: in which quadrant, or on which axis, a point lies, from the signs of its coordinates.
Beat: none.
Needs: a pair; the same six places offered every time (four quadrants, two axes); a wrong choice's note.
Props: none: words

## plotting-points

Order: state → see it hold for plotting (a method: Riya's walk); discover → state for the ordered pair (found when swapped numbers land in different places).

### plot-a-point  (o11; s3.3, def-plotting, s3.3-method, fig3.15)
Purpose: to plot (3, 5), walk 3 units along the x-axis from O to A, then 5 units in the y direction to P; Q (5, −4) the same way, downward. P is in quadrant I and Q in quadrant IV, from their signs. Volunteers set up the stalls from Riya's numbers.
Beat: volunteers set up the stalls from Riya's numbers.
Needs: empty axes; the student walks Riya from O along the x-axis and then up or down, and each point is marked as she reaches it. Then the student places R themselves, and Riya walks to wherever it was placed: right, she is at R; wrong, the board says where she is instead.
Props: coordinatePlane (places: stalls; walker; plotting)

### swap-the-numbers  (o12; example3, fig3.16, note-ordered-pair)
Purpose: Aman writes the snacks stall as (2, 5) instead of (5, 2), and it stands in the wrong place. Plotting (5, 0) and (0, 5), (5, 2) and (2, 5), (−3, 5) and (5, −3) shows that swapping the numbers moves the point unless they are equal; so (x, y) is an ordered pair.
Beat: Aman writes the snacks stall as (2, 5) instead of (5, 2), and it stands in the wrong place.
Needs: the student plots Example 3's eight points; each pair and its swap shown together; a pair with equal numbers, swapped, stays put.
Props: coordinatePlane (places: stalls; plotting)

### plot-from-a-table  (o11, o9; example4, fig3.17, ex3.3, ex3.3-q1, ex3.3-q2)
Purpose: points can come from a table and fall between grid lines, like (0, −3.5); the scale is chosen so every point fits, as with y = 8 in Exercise 3.3; a point's quadrant or axis can be told from its signs, then checked by plotting.
Beat: none: the book's table and exercise.
Needs: a table of x and y; the student plots each pair, including a decimal one between the marks; a choice of scale, first too small (a point falls off), then right.
Props: coordinatePlane (plotting: a table, a scale)

### treasure-trail  (o13; s3.3-method, example3)
Purpose: a treasure trail at the fair: each stall's clue gives the next stall's address, through all four quadrants and onto an axis, until the stall with Aman's lost kite. Every move is a plot, from where Riya stands; a wrong stop is told by its own address. The route is fixed, so every student finishes. (It takes the place of the textbook's dice game, Activity 2: a game anyone can finish, with nothing left to luck.)
Beat: a treasure trail at the fair, stall to stall, to Aman's lost kite.
Needs: the fair ground with about eight stalls, some at a swapped or sign-flipped address of a clue's; Riya walked with arrows from the gate at O; the clue shown, and the next one when she reaches its stall; at any other stall, its goods and address, and that it is not the clue's.
Props: coordinatePlane (places: stalls; walker)

### graphs-you-know  (o14; remark-graphs, s3.4, summary)
Purpose: a time-distance graph from earlier classes is the same plotting, with the axes called t and d instead of x and y: Riya's walk to Aman's house, a point each minute. The lesson ends with summary point 11.
Beat: Riya's walk to Aman's house, timed: a point each minute.
Needs: Riya's walk timed, its time and distance in a table, plotted as points on axes named t and d.
Props: coordinatePlane (walker, timed; plotting on axes named t and d)

### questions/plot-the-point  (o11, o12; s3.3-method, ex3.3-q2)
Purpose: plot a pair by dragging a dot there, halves between the marks included; the notes are the slips: the numbers swapped, x's sign the wrong way.
Beat: none.
Needs: empty axes; a dot dragged from O to the pair asked for.
Props: coordinatePlane (plotting)
