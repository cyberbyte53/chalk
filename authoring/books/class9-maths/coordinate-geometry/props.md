The library has nothing for a plane, a grid of places or dice (`look` at `library/props/`: basics, decimal and primes only), so this chapter adds one package, `library/props/coordinates`, with three props. The coordinate plane carries every board from lesson 2 on; its marks (numbers, axis names, quadrants, coordinates) are each a part a board turns on only once the lesson has defined them.

| Need | Boards | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Places in columns and rows, counted from 1: pick only the first number and every place with it lights up; pick both and one place is left; tap a place to read its pair, first number first. Drawn as streets with houses, as desks, or as street crossings. | street-and-house, seating-plan, cross-streets | new: `placeGrid` | The student's step is narrowing from one number to two, in a world with no origin, no zero and no negatives. The plane's axes aren't defined until lesson 2, and drawing desks on it would teach an origin too early. One prop with three looks (streets, desks, crossings) instead of three pictures. |
| A sheet with a dot; each clue the student picks shows where the dot could still be: a phrase ("upper half") a region, one distance a whole line, two distances one point. | dot-on-paper | new: `coordinatePlane` | The plane with its axes drawn as the sheet's left and bottom edges and no numbers or names yet. The "could still be here" region is the plane's own clue part, which comes back in lesson 3 (a point on the x-axis: every point at distance 0). |
| Two perpendicular lines beside the dot, the desk and the house; tilted perpendicular pairs; Descartes' portrait. | two-lines, two-number-lines | no prop | Pictures: nothing to do but look, and the next board hands the student the lines. |

| Two number lines, one horizontal and one vertical, that the student brings together until their zeroes meet; the crossing is named O. | two-number-lines | new: `coordinatePlane` | An action of the plane: the vertical line starts apart and moves until its zero meets the horizontal line's zero. |
