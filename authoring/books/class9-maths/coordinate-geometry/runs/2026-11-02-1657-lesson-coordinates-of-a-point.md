# Lesson 3: coordinates-of-a-point

## Boards (flow.md's order)

1. **distances-from-axes** (o6; s3.2-distances, fig3.10): Riya walks O→P, then O→Q on graph paper. P and Q carry dashed perpendiculars to both axes, and each distance is said with its direction.
2. **abscissa-and-ordinate** (def-abscissa, def-ordinate): the definitions first, then the student drags the point from P to Q and watches each sign flip.
3. **writing-coordinates** (def-coordinates, note-unique, remark-convention, epigraph):
   - (x, y), x first; (3, 4) is not P;
   - the order is an agreement, like Aman's address, with Lewis Carroll's "merely conventional signs";
   - the fair ground's plan, with two stalls to tap.
4. **reading-points** (example1, fig3.11): a worked example, one point per line.
5. **points-on-axes** (example2, rule-on-axes, rule-origin):
   - the point dragged onto each axis and onto O;
   - the y = 0 / x = 0 buttons light a whole axis;
   - Example 2, one point per line, E at (2/3, 0).
6. **signs-and-quadrants** (rule-signs, fig3.13): each quadrant's signs fill in once the point has been there; then "from the signs alone", which quadrant is (−3, 5).
7. **any-axes** (s3.2-perpendicular, fig3.6, remark-convention): O dragged and the axes turned by a ↻ handle. The point stays put and its coordinates, to 2 decimals, are written under the plane.
8. **find-the-point** (ex3.2-q2, fig3.14, s3.4): the coordinates of B, then the point at (−3, −5), then summary points 6–10.
