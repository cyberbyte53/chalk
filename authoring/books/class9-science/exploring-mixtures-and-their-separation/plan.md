# Exploring Mixtures and their Separation (class 9 science, chapter 5)

For class 9, NCERT Science, Chapter 5; English and Hindi, every string. Each board's
detail will be in [authoring/books/class9-science/exploring-mixtures-and-their-separation/boards.md](boards.md).

## Goal

A student can tell what kind of mixture something is, say how strong a solution is as a percentage,
and separate a mixture by the one property its parts differ in: how much dissolves, boiling point,
how fast it climbs paper, density, subliming or particle size.

## Objectives

- **o1** Tell a homogeneous mixture, or solution (the same throughout: the first sip as sweet as the last), from a heterogeneous one (its parts can be seen and may settle), for liquids and for gases (air is one, smoke and fog are the other).
- **o2** Say that an alloy (brass, bronze, steel) is a homogeneous mixture of metals, or a metal and a non-metal, made by melting them together, and that no physical method separates it.
- **o3** Name a solution's solute and solvent, and say that its concentration is how much solute is in a given amount of solvent or solution, and why the right amount matters (ORS, a pesticide spray).
- **o4** Find a solution's mass by mass percentage, the grams of solute in 100 g of solution, adding solute and solvent to get the solution's mass; give any part's share of a mixture that is not a solution the same way (a cake's dry mix, a food label); and work back from a percentage to an amount.
- **o5** Find a mass by volume and a volume by volume percentage, and choose among the three: mass by volume where a volume is easier to measure than a mass (a glucose drip), volume by volume where two miscible liquids are mixed (vinegar, a pesticide spray).
- **o6** Make a saturated solution by adding solute until no more dissolves, and say that solubility is the most that dissolves in 100 g (or 100 mL) of solvent at a given temperature, so that half the solvent takes half as much.
- **o7** Say that heating lets more of a solid dissolve but less of a gas.
- **o8** Read a substance's solubility at a temperature off its solubility curve or a table, and compare two substances by it.
- **o9** Find how much comes out when a hot saturated solution cools (the difference of the two solubilities), and say that it comes out as crystals, solids whose particles are in a regular pattern.
- **o10** Purify a solid by crystallization (saturate it hot, filter it hot, cool it slowly, collect the crystals), tell it from evaporating to dryness, and say why slow cooling gives larger crystals.
- **o11** Separate two miscible liquids, or a liquid from what is dissolved in it, by distillation (the flask, the condenser, the receiver), and say why their boiling points must differ by about 25 °C or more.
- **o12** Say that liquids whose boiling points are closer than that need fractional distillation, as crude oil does in a refinery.
- **o13** Separate the colours in an ink by paper chromatography, the spot above the solvent, and say that the solvent carries each part up the paper at its own speed.
- **o14** Separate two immiscible liquids with a separating funnel: the less dense liquid (less mass in the same volume, whatever the amounts) floats on top, and the lower one is drained first.
- **o15** Separate a substance that sublimes (camphor, naphthalene) from sand, naming sublimation and deposition.
- **o16** Say that a suspension's particles are big enough to see and settle with time, and that filtering may not clear the finest of them.
- **o17** Separate heavier particles fast by centrifugation, the heavier going outward, to the bottom of the tube (blood's cells from its plasma).
- **o18** Clear fine particles by coagulation: a coagulant such as alum clumps them so they settle.
- **o19** Say that a colloid (milk, blood) looks even and never settles, yet is a heterogeneous mixture, its particles big enough to scatter light; and name its dispersed phase and dispersion medium.
- **o20** Tell solutions, colloids and suspensions apart by particle size (under 1 nm, 1 to 1000 nm, over 1000 nm), and by what follows from it: seen or not, settles or not, held by a filter or not.
- **o21** Explain the Tyndall effect: a beam's path shows in a colloid or a suspension, never in a solution (sunlight through leaves, a stadium's floodlights).
- **o22** Choose the method for a mixture from the property its parts differ in, with dissolving, filtering and evaporating from earlier classes among them, and put methods in order for a mixture of three (sand, salt and naphthalene).

## Story

Riya and Aman (class 9 maths' friends, the same year) are at their school's camp by the sea, on duty
at the camp's kitchen and first-aid tent. Their mission: everything the camp drinks, eats and needs
when someone falls ill is made right, from what the river, the sea and the stores give them. Each
lesson is a stop on it: three glasses on the kitchen table, salt water, chalky water and a little
milk in water, look different and settle differently, and why stays open (1); Aman is ill and the
ORS must be just right (2); sugar for Riya's mishri stops dissolving in a little water, then dissolves again
when it is warmed (3); Riya's mishri grows from that syrup as it cools, while the salt in the sea pans comes as the water evaporates (4); drinking
water from sea water, and a spilt bottle of acetone in the science tent, go through a still (5);
Aman's jar labels run in the rain and the black ink splits into colours (6); mustard oil spills into
the water drum, and camphor for the mosquitoes into the sand (7); the river water stays muddy after
filtering, alum clears it, and the camp doctor's paperfuge finds malaria in a drop of blood (8); a
torch through the night's fog, and back on the kitchen table the beam shows in two of the three
glasses, which closes the question of the first stop (9); on the last day, the store's spilt sand, salt and naphthalene, and every mixture of the camp, go to the method that separates them (10). The camp's torch replaces Activity 5.1's
laser pointer. Lesson 1 takes in the alloy and the mixtures of gases, which the book meets among the
separations, so the sorting is done once; otherwise the book's order.

## Lessons

### kinds-of-mixtures  (o1, o2)
Goal: some mixtures are the same throughout and some are not. Discover → state.
Does: stirs salt, chalk and a little milk into three glasses, sips first and last, waits and watches
what settles, and leaves the milk glass as a question; sorts air, smoke, fog, soda, oil and water and brass as the same throughout or not.
Card: three glasses, clear, milky and chalky, the chalk settling.

### concentration  (o3, o4, o5)
Goal: how strong a solution is, as the grams or millilitres of solute in 100 of solution. Discover → state.
Does: makes ORS on a kitchen scale, spoon by spoon, watching its percentage, too weak and too strong;
reads a glucose drip's and a vinegar bottle's labels; mixes a pesticide spray in a measuring jug.
Card: a glass on a kitchen scale, its percentage beside it.

### solubility  (o6, o7, o8)
Goal: only so much dissolves, and how much depends on the temperature. Discover → state.
Does: adds sugar to a little water until grains stay at the bottom (a syrup); warms the glass and they dissolve; watches a warm
soda lose its gas; slides along two solubility curves and reads them.
Card: a glass with grains at the bottom, a thermometer in it.

### crystallization  (o9, o10)
Goal: a hot saturated solution, cooled, gives up its extra solute as pure crystals. Discover → state.
Does: cools a saturated solution of compound B from 60 °C to 40 °C, its point sliding down the curve
as 46 g come out as crystals; cools one slowly and one fast; follows sea water to salt in the pans,
where the water evaporates and cooling plays no part.
Card: blue crystals in a cooling beaker.

### distillation  (o11, o12)
Goal: heat a mixture, and the liquid that boils first leaves, then condenses. Discover → state.
Does: heats acetone and water, watches the thermometer hold near 56 °C while acetone drips into the
receiver, then climb; distils sea water to drinking water; tries alcohol and benzene, two degrees
apart, and both come over.
Card: a flask on a burner, a condenser, drops falling into a flask.

### paper-chromatography  (o13)
Goal: a solvent climbing paper carries each part of an ink at its own speed. Discover → state.
Does: dips a strip with a black spot, first too deep (the ink washes into the water), then right,
and watches black separate into colours; tries green food colour.
Card: a paper strip, a black spot spread into bands of colour.

### oil-water-and-camphor  (o14, o15)
Goal: parts that never dissolve in each other part by the one thing only one of them does: oil floats
on water, camphor turns straight into vapour and leaves the sand. Discover → state.
Does: lets oil and water settle in a separating funnel and drains the water, closing the tap at the
line; heats camphor and sand under a funnel and watches camphor collect on its wall.
Card: a separating funnel, yellow oil over water, beside a funnel over a dish, camphor on its wall.

### suspensions  (o16, o17, o18)
Goal: big particles settle, heavy ones can be spun down, fine ones clumped with alum. State → see it hold.
Does: waits and filters muddy river water and it stays cloudy; adds alum and the clumps sink; spins
a drop of blood in a paperfuge until the cells settle below the plasma.
Card: a beaker of muddy water, clumps sinking.

### colloids-and-light  (o19, o20, o21)
Goal: a colloid's particles are between a solution's and a suspension's: they never settle, but they
scatter light. Discover → state.
Does: shines a torch through the three glasses of the first stop and through fog; sets particle size
and watches what settles, what a filter holds and whether the beam shows; fills the table of the
three kinds.
Card: a torch beam through a glass of milky water, its path glowing.

### choosing-a-method  (o22)
Goal: a mixture is separated by the property its parts differ in, one method for each difference.
State → see it hold.
Does: takes sand, salt and naphthalene apart in the order that works (sublime, dissolve and filter,
evaporate), and sees filtering before dissolving leave the salt with the sand: each step needs the
difference it uses to be there, and more than one order can work; sends each of the camp's mixtures to its method, with the
difference it uses.
Card: a dish of sand, salt and naphthalene, three arrows to a funnel, a filter and an evaporating dish.

## Props

- **new** `mixture` (a new package, `library/props/mixtures/`), a host: a mixture made of parts, each with its particle size, density, solubility at each temperature, boiling point, whether it sublimes and how fast it climbs paper, shown in the apparatus a board chooses (a glass on a kitchen scale with millilitre marks, a separating funnel, a still with its thermometer, a dish under a funnel, a paper strip in a jar, a paperfuge's tube). The student adds by the spoon or the millilitre, stirs, waits, filters, warms and cools, evaporates, adds alum, shines a torch, spins, opens a tap or dips a strip, and each part moves by its own property, so whatever is tried comes out true. Why: the library has nothing for science, and the chapter's one idea, that a method acts on the one property the parts differ in, is shown best by one mixture that every method acts on truthfully.
- **extend** `coordinatePlane`: curves, a named line through given points, with a point that slides along it showing both readings (the dashed lines it already draws to the axes). Why: a solubility curve is a graph on two axes with other names; reading 287 and 241 is reading a point, which it already does.
- **reuse** `choice` and `goal`, for the questions; a question answered by doing (saturate it, drain the water, close the tap at the line) is a `goal` on `mixture`.

Fractional distillation and the refinery, the alloys and the blood-donation layers of Fig. 5.22 are
pictures and words, with no prop.
