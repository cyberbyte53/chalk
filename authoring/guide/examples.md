# Examples, by principle

A few situations per principle, from different subjects. Copy the idea, never the subject or the wording.
*Seen* marks something that happened in a lesson we made and was corrected.

## 1. Explain before you use

- *Seen:* a tapped desk was marked P or Q, letters the board never introduced; they also hid which desk was
  which, the very thing the next question asked.
- A lens diagram labels F and 2F before saying what a focus is.
- "pH" appears on a scale before the board says what it measures.

## 2. Make them need it first

- *Seen:* a board said a place needs two numbers before the student had any reason to think one wasn't
  enough. Rebuilt so one number brings them to a street with five houses, and the second number is the
  answer to "which one?".
- *Seen:* a board asked "how should these new streets be numbered?" while the map already printed the
  numbers.
- "Which beaker turns litmus red?" asked while each beaker is labelled acid or base.

## 3. Cut what isn't needed

- *Seen:* a seating plan drew the teacher's desk, which nothing on the board was about. It went, with every
  mention of the teacher.
- *Seen:* a figure kept from the textbook (lines drawn tilted) that no idea on the board needed. Cut: the
  textbook's ideas stay, not every figure.
- A cell diagram labels twelve organelles on a board about the nucleus.

## 4. Let them do it, and let the prop tell the truth

- *Seen:* a treasure hunt accepted a stall other than the one the clue named. Only the right stall counts now.
- *Seen:* the student could tap any point but the walker wouldn't go there unless it was the answer. Now it
  goes wherever they tap, and the board says whether it's right.
- A circuit gives the true current for any wiring the student makes, not only the wiring the lesson expects.

## 5. Test what the lesson taught, the way it taught it

- *Seen:* a lesson taught by walking on a map, then tested with a word problem about a city plan. The
  question moved onto the map.
- *Seen:* a question's prompt stated the rule it was testing ("the column comes first"), giving the answer
  away.
- A wrong option marked only "wrong", where "you read the scale from the wrong end" would have taught.
- Equations balanced by moving molecules in the lesson, then tested by typing an equation.

## 6. Keep the world consistent

- *Seen:* the stalls of a fair stood in one place in one lesson and somewhere else in the next.
- A character who is in class 9 in one chapter talks about class 10 exams in the next.
- "Speed" on one board, "velocity" on the next, for the same thing.
