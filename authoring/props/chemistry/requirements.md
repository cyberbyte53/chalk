# What class 9 and 10 chemistry asks of props

Read from the seven chemistry chapters' sources (mixtures, the atom, atoms and molecules, chemical reactions, acids and bases, metals and non-metals, carbon), with [authoring/taste/guides/props.md](../../taste/guides/props.md), [library/catalogue.md](../../../library/catalogue.md) and the mixtures plan, [authoring/books/class9-science/exploring-mixtures-and-their-separation/plan.md](../../books/class9-science/exploring-mixtures-and-their-separation/plan.md). The library has nothing for science yet: only text, the two question props and the coordinate plane.

## 1. What a student does and sees

**Mix, dissolve and separate.** Mixtures (all of it); atoms and molecules (salt and sugar dissolve in water, camphor and naphthalene in kerosene); carbon (oil and water shaken with and without soap; soap in hard and soft water, foam or scum); acids (copper sulphate crystals hold water). Stir salt, chalk and milk into glasses and watch what settles; saturate, warm, cool into crystals; distil, run a paper strip, drain a separating funnel, sublime camphor, add alum, spin blood.

**Weigh and measure.** Mixtures (a solution's percentage on a kitchen scale); atoms and molecules (salt dissolved on a balance, mass unchanged; vinegar and baking soda in an open flask, the reading drops, with a balloon on it, it doesn't; two solutions mixed, the same mass after); reactions and acids (the beaker warms or cools: quick lime in water, acid into water).

**React and watch.** Reactions (magnesium burns to white ash; yellow precipitate from two clear solutions; zinc in acid bubbles and warms; green crystals heated turn brown; lead nitrate gives brown fumes; water split by a current, twice as much gas at one end; silver chloride greys in the sun; an iron nail turns brown in blue copper sulphate and the blue fades; copper blackens on heating). Acids (metal with acid, carbonate with acid, copper oxide dissolves blue). Metals (metals with air, cold water, hot water, steam and acid at different speeds; a metal in another's salt solution, displaced or not; three tubes of nails, only the one with air and water rusts). Carbon (compounds burn clean or sooty; the burner's air hole gives a blue or yellow flame; purple permanganate fades in warm ethanol until there is too much; sodium fizzes in ethanol; acid and alcohol warmed smell fruity).

**Test it.** Acids (litmus, phenolphthalein, methyl orange and turmeric on many solutions; onion and clove smell; pH paper on lemon, saliva, soil; phenolphthalein pink fading as acid goes in drop by drop, back with more base; dry and wet litmus in HCl gas). Gas tests in reactions, acids, metals and carbon (hydrogen pops at a flame, carbon dioxide turns lime water milky, then clear again with more). Conductivity with a bulb and two nails in atoms and molecules, acids and metals (acid solutions light it, glucose and alcohol don't; salt solution does, solid salt doesn't; metals do, non-metals mostly don't). Metals by touch and tool: shine after sandpaper, cut, hammer flat, a wax pin drops off a heated wire.

**Build atoms and count their particles.** The atom (protons, neutrons, electrons; atomic number and mass number; shells filled 2, 8, 8; valence electrons and valency; isotopes and a weighted average mass; isobars; most alpha particles pass through gold foil). Metals and carbon repeat the shells to explain ions and carbon's four bonds.

**Join atoms.** Atoms and molecules (hydrogen, chlorine, oxygen, hydrogen chloride and water by sharing; sodium gives an electron to chlorine; the salt crystal). Metals (sodium chloride and magnesium chloride by transfer). Carbon (single, double and triple bonds; methane; chains of one to six carbons; two shapes of butane; rings; functional groups on a chain; a family that grows by one carbon at a time; soap's two ends around oil).

**Read and write formulas and equations.** The atom (symbols, a capital then a small letter; the mass and atomic numbers on a symbol). Atoms and molecules (formulas by crossing valencies over, brackets for a repeated group ion, molecular and formula-unit mass, naming). Reactions (word equations, skeletal to balanced by counting each element, never changing a formula; state symbols; conditions over the arrow; kinds of reaction; oxidised and reduced). Acids (salts and the acid and base they come from). Carbon (names from chain length and group; homologous series by adding one carbon and two hydrogens).

**Read a scale or a graph.** Mixtures (solubility curves); acids (the pH scale, 0 to 14); metals (the reactivity series as a ladder); carbon (boiling points rising along a series).

## 2. The things the props would model

- **A substance**: name, formula, colour, state at room temperature, density, particle size (in a mixture), melting and boiling point, solubility in water and in kerosene at each temperature, whether it sublimes, how fast paper carries it, its pH (or acid or base, and how strong), whether it gives ions in water (so conducts), and for a metal its place in the reactivity series. Needed by mixing, weighing, reacting, testing and graphs.
- **A portion in a vessel**: a substance, how much (grams or millilitres), and where it is: dissolved, solid at the bottom, a layer, a gas over it or escaped, crystals, on paper. The vessel has a temperature and is open or closed. Mixing, weighing, reacting, testing.
- **A reaction**: which substances, under what condition (heat, light, current, water, a catalyst), give which products, with what a student sees (gas, precipitate, colour, warm or cold) and how fast. Reacting, testing, equations.
- **An atom**: protons, neutrons, electrons in shells; from these its element, atomic number, mass number, charge, valence electrons. Building atoms, joining atoms.
- **An ion**: an atom (or a group of atoms) with electrons gained or lost, so a charge. Joining atoms, formulas, testing (conductivity, H⁺ and OH⁻ in acids and bases).
- **A bond**: shared pairs (one, two, three) between two atoms, or a transfer. Joining atoms, carbon.
- **A molecule or formula unit**: atoms and bonds; from them its formula, its mass, its name, saturated or not, its isomers. Joining atoms, formulas and equations.
- **An equation**: formulas with counts in front, two sides, and each element's count on each side. Equations, weighing (mass on both sides).

## 3. Candidate props

Four new props and one extension cover nearly all of it.

1. **The bench** (extend: the mixtures plan's `mixture`, grown). Vessels of substances on a bench, each substance acting by its own properties, and reactions as data: when the right substances meet under the right condition they become the products and show their signs. Apparatus are parts a board turns on: glass on a scale, still, funnel, paper strip, centrifuge, test tubes, burner (with its air hole), balance with a flask and balloon, thermometer, dropper of indicator and pH paper, delivery tube to lime water or a soap bubble and a flame, bulb-and-nails circuit, two electrodes for a current. Serves mixing, weighing, reacting and testing, in all seven chapters but the atom. It earns its place because whatever the student tries comes out true: the wrong order of separations leaves salt with sand, the open flask loses mass, copper in iron sulphate does nothing.
2. **An atom** (new). A nucleus the student fills with protons and neutrons and shells he fills with electrons, reading element, atomic and mass numbers, charge, configuration and valence electrons as he goes; isotopes are the same atom with a neutron more. Serves building atoms (the atom chapter) and the start of joining atoms (atoms and molecules, metals, carbon).
3. **Molecules** (new). Atoms shown by their outer shells; the student shares pairs or moves an electron until each is full, and reads the formula, name and mass; in carbon the same atoms make chains, branches, rings, double and triple bonds, with a dot view and a line view. Serves joining atoms and formulas (atoms and molecules, metals, carbon).
4. **An equation** (new). Formulas as tiles on two sides; the student changes only the numbers in front, and each element's count on each side shows, with the atoms drawn as balls beside it. Also writes a formula by crossing two ions' charges. Serves equations and formulas (atoms and molecules, reactions, acids, carbon).
5. **The coordinate plane** (extend, as the mixtures plan already says): a curve through given points with a point sliding along it. Serves solubility curves, and boiling point against chain length.
6. **`choice` and `goal`** (reuse) for questions; a sorting question (drag things into two or three bins: metal or non-metal, acid or base, kind of reaction, kind of mixture) comes up in every chapter and may be worth adding to the basics.

**Better as pictures.** The history of the atom models, the cathode ray tube, Dalton's symbols, the nuclear plant and the uses of isotopes; the refinery; the hammer, knife, bell and shine of metals (sorting them is a question, not a prop); ores to metal, roasting, calcination, the thermit weld, electrolytic refining and anodising; the chlor-alkali products and the uses of salts; diamond, graphite and the football molecule; the salt crystal's lattice; micelles; coal and oil forming; the fire extinguisher; health and soil pH. Each only shows; nothing in it is a step the student takes. The pH scale and the reactivity series are a labelled strip, drawn, with the bench doing the testing. Two borderline cases: the gold foil (firing alpha particles and seeing most pass is a real mechanism, an optional part of the atom), and an electron jumping between shells (words and a picture are enough).

**Real choices.**
- *One bench or several.* One bench with reactions as data means each chapter adds substances and reactions, not code, and the student meets one lab all year; the cost is one large prop whose parts must stay independent. Separate props (mixing, reacting, testing) are each simpler, but a carbonate fizzing into lime water is a reaction, a gas, a test and a weighing at once, and they would have to share the substance list and the vessel anyway.
- *Atom and molecules as one prop or two.* One gives continuity, the sodium atom he built is the one that gives its electron away; two keeps each simple, the atom with its nucleus, molecules with outer shells only. Carbon's chains could even be a third, but they use the same atoms and bonds, so they belong in molecules.
- *Particles in the bench or not.* A zoom showing the particles in a vessel (H⁺ ions in acid, ions moving to the nails, sizes in a colloid) ties the bench to the atom; it can come later as one part.

## 4. The shape the `mixture` prop should take

So that the later chapters extend it:

- **Named and placed for the bench**, in `library/props/chemistry/`, not `mixtures/`: it will hold reactions and tests too.
- **Substances as data, shared**: a substance list, each with the properties in section 2, of which mixtures fills only what it needs (particle size, density, solubility by temperature, boiling point, sublimes, paper speed). A board names substances from the list; it never carries its own copy. Later chapters add fields (formula, colour, pH, ions, reactivity) and entries.
- **Contents as portions**: each a substance, an amount and where it is, so mass is counted the same everywhere and conservation holds by construction; an open vessel lets gas go, a closed one keeps it.
- **The vessel's own state**: temperature, open or closed, what it sits on or in.
- **Generic actions**: add, pour, stir, heat, cool, wait, filter, and one step in which each portion acts by its properties; later a reaction step runs after it, matching reactions from data, without changing the first.
- **Apparatus as parts**, each turned on by a board; the later ones (test tube, delivery tube, dropper, circuit, electrodes, balance with balloon) join as more parts.
- **What he sees, in words**: each step reports what changed (grains stay at the bottom, the reading holds at 56 °C), so Jugnu and the questions read it the same way for reactions later (bubbles, a yellow solid, the tube warms).
