# Class 10 Maths, Ch13: Surface Areas and Volumes (plan)

Source: NCERT jemh113 (2020-21, the full edition: combinations, conversion of solids, the frustum of a cone, and Exercise 13.5 (optional)).

Structure: theory-first, like the other maths chapters. Every lesson runs:
1. Hook (story)
2. Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props)
3. See it in life (story, explained)
4. Practice (examples, your turn, mission log)

Hindi with every string. Sentence first, then the maths on its own line. π = 22/7 unless NCERT says 3.14 or "in terms of π"; every prop preset carries its own π.

## Mission: The Weekend Stall
The Sunday market is starting a children's row, and Riya and Kabir get a stall. They will sell Grandpa's wooden toys (lattus, a toy rocket, blocks with a dome) and Mom's sweets (gulab jamun, kulfi in cones, juice). Grandpa's village sends jaggery made in moulds.

Every question the stall raises is one of the chapter's:
- How much paint does a lattu need? Paint covers the outside: surface area. The faces that touch when two solids are joined are not painted.
- How much wood, syrup or juice? That is volume, and volumes of joined solids add.
- How many kulfi cones does one can fill? Melting, pouring and reshaping keep the volume: conversion of solids.
- What shape is Grandpa's bucket, a jaggery mould, a glass? A cone with its top cut off: the frustum.

Checked numbers (the props' tests check them all):
- the lattu: 2πr² + πrl ≈ 39.6 cm² of paint (Example 1);
- the rocket: orange 63.585 cm², yellow 195.465 cm² (Example 3, π = 3.14);
- a gulab jamun ≈ 25.05 cm³; the syrup in 45 of them ≈ 338 cm³ (Ex 13.2 Q3);
- the juice glass: looks like 196.25 cm³, holds 163.54 cm³ (Example 6);
- the kulfi can (r 6, h 15) fills 10 cones with scoops (Ex 13.3 Q5);
- a jaggery mould holds ≈ 11,641.67 cm³ ≈ 13.97 kg of molasses (Example 13).

The roadmap has four stops:
1. Painting joined solids (surface area).
2. Filling joined solids (volume).
3. Melt it, pour it, make something new (conversion).
4. The cut cone (frustum).

A challenge stop and the finale end the chapter: market day, the stall sells out.

## Lessons
1. **The solids we know** (13.1; recap of Class IX)
   - Hook: the empty stall. Kabir lists what they will sell; Riya sees cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres everywhere.
   - Learn:
     - the solids: cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, hemisphere; their measurements r, h, l, a, l × b × h;
     - the slant height of a cone, l = √(r² + h²);
     - curved (lateral) surface area, total surface area and volume of each, with the net that explains the area (the cylinder's rectangle 2πr × h, the cone's sector of radius l with arc 2πr, the sphere's four circles);
     - "total" means every face; "curved" means the round part only.
   - See it in life: Grandpa's cylinder of wood (r 3.5, h 10): curved surface 220 cm², volume 385 cm³.
   - Practice: quick recall questions, one per solid (no NCERT exercise here).
2. **Surface area of a combination of solids** (13.2; Examples 1–4; Exercise 13.1)
   - Hook: Grandpa's lattus. "How much paint for one?" Kabir adds both solids' total surfaces. Riya: "The flat circle where they join is inside. Nobody paints that."
   - Learn:
     - TSA of a combination = the parts' curved surfaces, plus only the faces still showing (NCERT's tub and the capsule);
     - when a smaller solid sits on a bigger face, take the covered circle away (Example 2: 6a² − πr² + 2πr²);
     - Example 1 (the lattu), Example 2 (the block), Example 3 (the rocket in two colours, π = 3.14), Example 4 (the bird-bath, 33,000 cm²);
     - hollows: the inside of a cavity is surface too (Ex 13.1 Q8, Q9).
   - See it in life: paint for the stall's lattus, and Kabir paints the rocket orange and yellow.
   - Practice: Exercise 13.1 Q1–9 (two cubes 160; vessel 572; toy 214.5; cube with dome 332.5; capsule 220 mm²; tent 44 m², ₹22,000; cone cavity 17.6 ≈ 18; scooped article 374).
3. **Volume of a combination of solids** (13.3; Examples 5–7; Exercise 13.2)
   - Hook: Mom's gulab jamuns. "How much syrup do I need?" Volume, unlike area, just adds: no faces to drop.
   - Learn:
     - the volume of a combination = the sum of the parts' volumes; cut-outs are taken away;
     - Example 5 (Shanta's shed, a cuboid with half a cylinder);
     - Example 6 (the juice glass: apparent and actual capacity);
     - Example 7 (a toy inside the cylinder round it, π = 3.14).
   - See it in life: syrup for 45 gulab jamuns ≈ 338 cm³; the stall's juice glasses hold less than they look.
   - Practice: Exercise 13.2 Q1–8 (cone on hemisphere π cm³; Rachel's model 66; gulab jamun 338; pen stand; lead shots 100; iron pole; water left 1,131,428.57 cm³; flask 346.51).
4. **Converting a solid into another** (13.4; Examples 8–11; Exercise 13.3)
   - Hook: a tall can of kulfi and a stack of cones. "How many cones will the can fill?"
   - Learn:
     - melting, recasting, pouring, digging and spreading keep the volume: volume before = volume after;
     - one unknown (a radius, a height, how many) comes out of that equation;
     - Example 8 (clay cone to sphere, r = 6), Example 9 (Selvi's sump and tank, 47.5 cm, π = 3.14), Example 10 (rod drawn into a wire, thickness 1/15 cm), Example 11 (emptying a hemispherical tank, 16.5 min);
     - flow problems: water in a pipe or canal is a long cylinder or cuboid (speed × time is its length).
   - See it in life: the kulfi can fills 10 cones with scoops on top.
   - Practice: Exercise 13.3 Q1–9 (h = 2.744; R = 12; platform 2.5 m; embankment; 10 cones; 400 coins; heap r 36, l = 12√13; canal; tank filled in 100 min).
5. **Frustum of a cone** (13.5; Examples 12–14; Exercise 13.4)
   - Hook: the village yard. Grandpa's bucket and the jaggery moulds are cones with the top cut off.
   - Learn:
     - Activity 1: cut a clay cone parallel to its base; what is left is a **frustum**;
     - the frustum as big cone minus small cone; h₂ from similar triangles, h₂ = h·r₂/(r₁ − r₂);
     - Example 12 worked the long way (h₁ = 60, h₂ = 15, V = 48,510 cm³), then the formulas:
       V = ⅓πh(r₁² + r₂² + r₁r₂), CSA = π(r₁ + r₂)l with l = √(h² + (r₁ − r₂)²), TSA = CSA + πr₁² + πr₂²;
     - Example 13 (the jaggery moulds), Example 14 (the bucket on a hollow base).
   - See it in life: one mould holds ≈ 11,641.67 cm³, about 13.97 kg of molasses; the stall's glasses are frusta too (Ex 13.4 Q1, 102.67 cm³).
   - Practice: Exercise 13.4 Q1–5 (glass; perimeters given; the fez; the milk container, π = 3.14; the cone cut at half height drawn into wire).
6. **Challenge and finale** (Exercise 13.5, optional; the Summary)
   - Hook: market day. Grandpa's last puzzles while the customers arrive.
   - Learn: Exercise 13.5 (the wire wound round a cylinder, the hollow flask with a neck, the coins, the glass of water and the marbles, the bottle with a frustum neck), each as one of the four ideas.
   - Finale: the stall is busy and sells out; the four stops are recapped.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-solids)
- **solidViewer**: one basic solid drawn from a little above with r, h, l labelled, or opened into its net.
  - Turn (four views), open and close the net, step the measurements, pick a measure.
  - Under it: the formula, the numbers put in, the answer (curved, total, volume).
- **comboBuilder**: a combination from the chapter (19 presets: Examples 1–4, 6, 7; Ex 13.1 Q1–4, 6–9; Ex 13.2 Q1–3, 7, 8).
  - Pull the parts apart and join them (the joined faces disappear).
  - The surface area or volume built up term by term, each term the part's own formula; faces hidden and hollows cut out are taken away; then the total.
- **meltRecast**: converting one solid into another (11 presets: Examples 8–10; Ex 13.3 Q1–3, 5–7, 9; Ex 13.2 Q5).
  - Steps: before (its volume); melted; the new solid with its unknown; solved.
  - The new solid fills up (still under reduced motion); "× n" for counts.
- **frustumCut**: a cone cut into a frustum.
  - Steps: the whole cone; the cutting plane; the small cone lifted off; the frustum with r₁, r₂, h, l.
  - Lines: h₂ from similar triangles, then volume, curved or total surface as big cone minus small cone, equal to the frustum formula.
- **solidsScene**: story pictures in the family's look: Stall (empty), Workshop (Grandpa's lattus), RocketPaint, Kitchen (gulab jamun), KulfiCan, JuiceGlasses, Jaggery (bucket and moulds), StallDay (finale).
