# Class 10 Science, Ch2: Acids, Bases and Salts (plan)

Source: NCERT jesc102 (2020-21, the full edition: keep everything). Structure: theory-first, like Light L1 and Ch1. Each lesson runs Hook (story) → Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props) → See it in life (story, explained) → Practice (examples, NCERT in-text questions and exercises, your turn, mission log). Hindi with every string.

## Mission: Kabir's birthday party
Diwali is over; next is Kabir's birthday party. Riya has promised the guests a **kitchen-chemistry magic show**, and every act (and every party mishap) is a piece of this chapter:
- **potions that change colour:** indicators (red cabbage juice, litmus, turmeric);
- **the curry stain on Kabir's white shirt** that turns red when scrubbed with soap (NCERT's own opener);
- **Grandpa's acidity after too much cake:** lemon juice, vinegar or baking soda? (NCERT's opening question);
- **the fluffy cake:** baking powder;
- **a bee sting in the garden:** baking soda paste;
- **sweets and teeth:** tooth decay below pH 5.5;
- **the fire-extinguisher finale:** soda-acid;
- **a plaster-of-Paris handprint** of Kabir's hand as a keepsake.

The finale is the party night: every act worked, and Riya can explain why.

Roadmap stops (one per lesson): (1) acid, base or neither? (2) acids with metals and carbonates; (3) acids and bases cancel out; (4) what all acids share: ions; (5) how strong: dilution and pH; (6) pH in living things; (7) salts; (8) chemicals from common salt; (9) salts with water inside: crystals and plaster of Paris.

## Lessons
1. **Acids, bases and indicators**
   - Hook: party planning; Riya's magic show; the curry stain.
   - Learn:
     - Class 9 recap (sour = acid, bitter = base; never taste lab chemicals);
     - indicator defined; litmus (purple solution, from lichen; red/blue papers);
     - Activity 2.1: Table 2.1 filled by the learner with red/blue litmus, phenolphthalein and methyl orange on HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, CH₃COOH, NaOH, Ca(OH)₂, KOH, Mg(OH)₂, NH₄OH;
     - natural indicators (turmeric, red cabbage, Hydrangea, Petunia, Geranium); olfactory indicators (Activity 2.2: onion, vanilla, clove).
   - See it in life: the curry stain explained (turmeric + basic soap); red-cabbage potions for the magic show.
   - Practice: in-text Q1 (only red litmus, three tubes); group activity (beetroot/red cabbage indicator).
2. **How acids and bases react with metals and carbonates**
   - Learn:
     - Activity 2.3: Zn + dil. H₂SO₄; hydrogen through soap solution; the candle pop. Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen; salt defined.
     - Activity 2.4: Zn + NaOH → sodium zincate + H₂ (not every metal does this).
     - Activity 2.5: Na₂CO₃ / NaHCO₃ + HCl → CO₂ through lime water (milky), then excess CO₂ (clears again). Limestone, chalk and marble are all CaCO₃. Carbonate/hydrogencarbonate + acid → salt + CO₂ + water.
   - See it in life: why the baking soda "volcano" fizzes; egg shells in vinegar (Exercise 2).
   - Practice: in-text Q2–Q3 (metal compound A → CaCl₂); Exercise 5 equations, balanced with the equationBalancer.
3. **Neutralisation, and the oxides**
   - Learn:
     - Activity 2.6: NaOH + phenolphthalein (pink); HCl drop by drop → colourless; NaOH again → pink. Neutralisation defined: base + acid → salt + water.
     - Activity 2.7: CuO + HCl → blue-green CuCl₂. Metal oxide + acid → salt + water, so metal oxides are basic oxides.
     - Section 2.1.6: CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂, so non-metal oxides are acidic.
   - See it in life: Grandpa's acidity, so baking soda, not lemon or vinegar; why curd isn't kept in brass/copper (in-text Q1).
   - Practice: Exercise 3 (10 mL NaOH : 8 mL HCl, so 20 mL : 16 mL); Exercise 14.
4. **What all acids and all bases have in common**
   - Learn:
     - Activity 2.8: conductivity (HCl, H₂SO₄ light the bulb; glucose and alcohol don't). Ion recap: the current is carried by ions; acids give H⁺.
     - Activity 2.9: dry vs wet HCl gas on litmus. H⁺ needs water: HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻; hydronium.
     - Bases give OH⁻; alkali defined (a base that dissolves in water); neutralisation as H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.
   - See it in life: why the potion trick needs water; Exercise 7 (distilled vs rain water).
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q3; Exercise 6, 8.
5. **How strong? Dilution and the pH scale**
   - Learn:
     - Activity 2.10: dissolving is exothermic; acid into water, never water into acid (the warning sign); dilution defined (fewer ions per volume).
     - Universal indicator; the pH scale from 0 to 14 (7 neutral; more H⁺ means lower pH; Fig 2.6).
     - Activity 2.11: Table 2.2 / Fig 2.7 with real substances.
     - Strong vs weak acids (HCl vs CH₃COOH, Exercise 10: magnesium fizzes harder in HCl).
   - See it in life: testing the party drinks, cake batter and soap on universal paper.
   - Practice: in-text Q5–Q6; Exercise 1, 9 (A–E: 4, 1, 11, 7, 9).
6. **pH in everyday life**
   - Learn:
     - our body works at pH 7.0–7.8;
     - acid rain below 5.6, and rivers; Venus's sulphuric acid clouds;
     - soil pH (Activity 2.12, and lime for acidic soil);
     - the stomach's HCl and antacids (milk of magnesia);
     - tooth decay below 5.5 and basic toothpaste;
     - stings (bee, nettle: methanoic acid) and their remedies (baking soda, dock leaf);
     - Table 2.3: natural acids.
   - See it in life: Kabir's sweets and his teeth; the bee sting in the garden.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q4 (pH 6 vs 8; the farmer and lime); Exercise 11 (milk to curd), Exercise 12 (the milkman's baking soda).
7. **Salts: families and their pH**
   - Learn:
     - salt recap (from neutralisation);
     - Activity 2.13 (formulae; parent acid and base; families by the same positive or negative radical);
     - Activity 2.14 / Table 2.4: strong + strong gives neutral, strong acid + weak base gives acidic, strong base + weak acid gives basic;
     - common salt; rock salt; the Dandi March.
   - See it in life: why baking soda is basic and table salt is neutral.
   - Practice: family-spotting and pH-of-a-salt questions.
8. **Chemicals from common salt**
   - Learn:
     - brine and the chlor-alkali process (2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂; anode/cathode; Fig 2.8 uses);
     - bleaching powder (Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂; its uses);
     - baking soda (made from NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ + NH₃; heated → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂; baking powder with tartaric acid; antacid; soda-acid extinguisher);
     - washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; its uses, including hard water).
   - See it in life: the fluffy cake; Mom's laundry; the extinguisher finale for the show.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q4; Exercise 15.
9. **Are salt crystals really dry? Water of crystallisation**
   - Learn:
     - Activity 2.15: blue CuSO₄·5H₂O heated turns white with droplets; add water and it's blue again. Water of crystallisation defined; Na₂CO₃·10H₂O isn't "wet".
     - Gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O; heated at 373 K gives Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O (why "½": two formula units share one water); with water it sets back to gypsum; its uses.
   - See it in life: Kabir's handprint keepsake; why PoP is stored airtight (Exercise 13).
   - Practice: in-text Q5; finale: party night, every act explained (mission complete).

## Props (added to @chalk/prop-chemistry, in new files)
- **acidIndicator**: watch-glasses of samples; pick an indicator (red/blue litmus paper, litmus solution, phenolphthalein, methyl orange, turmeric, red cabbage, universal; olfactory onion, vanilla, clove) and drop it on a sample; Table 2.1 fills in as you test; derived acid/base/neutral.
- **phScale**: the 0–14 strip in universal-indicator colours, H⁺/OH⁻ bars (Fig 2.6), everyday substances (Table 2.2 / Fig 2.7) to pick, a probe to drag; optional life bands (body 7.0–7.8, tooth decay < 5.5, acid rain < 5.6).
- **neutraliser**: Activity 2.6 as a burette-and-flask: NaOH + phenolphthalein, add acid drop by drop (or base back), with the colour, a pH readout and an optional H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O picture; a set "acid needed per mL of base" for Exercise 3.
- **acidBench**: the chapter's experiments, played once, with a second step where the book has one:
  - zinc + acid (hydrogen through soap, candle pop) and zinc + NaOH;
  - carbonate + acid through lime water (milky, then clear with excess CO₂);
  - CuO + HCl;
  - dry vs wet HCl gas on litmus;
  - acid into water vs water into acid;
  - Mg in HCl vs acetic acid;
  - copper sulphate crystals heated and re-wetted;
  - gypsum to plaster of Paris and back;
  - the soda-acid extinguisher.
- **conductivityTester**: Activity 2.8, with nails in a beaker, battery, bulb and switch; pick a solution (acids, alkalis, glucose, alcohol, distilled vs rain water, salt water); an ion close-up (H₃O⁺, Cl⁻, Na⁺, OH⁻ vs uncharged glucose).
- **saltFamily**: a cation × anion grid of salts; tap a salt for its parent acid and base (strong or weak) and its pH nature; tap an ion to light up its family.
- **saltFactory**: the common-salt map (brine → NaOH, Cl₂, H₂ → bleaching powder; NaCl → baking soda → washing soda); tap a product for its equation and uses; the chlor-alkali cell picture.
