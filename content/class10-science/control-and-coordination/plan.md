# Class 10 Science, Ch7: Control and Coordination (plan)

Source: NCERT jesc107 (2020-21, full edition; keep everything). Structure: theory-first, like Light L1. Each lesson is Hook (story) → Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props) → See it in life (story, explained) → Practice (examples, NCERT questions, your turn, mission log). Hindi with every string.

## Mission: the "Fastest Hands" stall at the school science fair
Riya's class has a stall at the science fair: "How does your body know what to do?". Kabir wants to beat everyone at the ruler-drop reaction game. Each lesson builds one exhibit:
- a neuron model
- the ruler-drop game, with a reflex vs thinking chart
- a brain map
- Grandpa's chhui-mui (touch-me-not) and a bean seedling in a shoebox with a window
- the hormones poster
The finale is fair day: Riya's stage fright before presenting is adrenaline, explained live. Family details stay consistent with the heredity chapter: Grandpa's terrace garden, and Grandpa's iodised salt / insulin as NCERT's examples.

## Lessons (NCERT order)
1. **Responding to change** (7 intro, 7.1)
   - why living things need control and coordination
   - stimulus → response; receptors (gustatory, olfactory)
   - the neuron: dendrite → cell body → axon → nerve ending, electrical impulse; synapse with a chemical across the gap; neuromuscular junction
   - Activity 7.1: taste with the nose blocked
2. **Reflex actions** (7.1.1)
   - "without thinking"; why thinking via the brain is too slow for a flame
   - reflex arc: receptor → sensory neuron → relay in spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle; the input also reaches the brain
   - bright light and the pupil
   - why reflex arcs evolved
3. **The brain and how action happens** (7.1.2–7.1.4)
   - CNS (brain + spinal cord) and PNS (cranial and spinal nerves)
   - voluntary, involuntary and reflex actions
   - fore-brain: thinking, sensory and association areas, motor areas, hunger centre
   - mid-brain and hind-brain: medulla for involuntary actions (blood pressure, salivation, vomiting); cerebellum for precision, posture and balance
   - protection: skull, fluid, vertebral column
   - muscles shorten because special proteins change shape and arrangement; voluntary vs involuntary muscle
4. **How plants move** (7.2, 7.2.1, 7.2.2)
   - no nerves, no muscles
   - touch-me-not: fast, no growth, cells change shape by changing their water
   - tendrils coil (the touched side grows slower)
   - tropisms, towards or away from the stimulus, with Activity 7.2 (beans in a box with a window, then turned): phototropism, geotropism, hydrotropism, chemotropism (pollen tube)
   - fast vs slow movements
5. **Plant hormones** (end of 7.2)
   - why chemical messages: electrical impulses reach only connected cells and need reset time; chemicals diffuse to every cell, slowly but steadily
   - hormone defined
   - auxin: made at the shoot tip, diffuses to the shady side, cells there grow longer, so the shoot bends towards light
   - gibberellins (stem growth), cytokinins (cell division, fruits and seeds), abscisic acid (stops growth, wilting)
6. **Hormones in animals** (7.3)
   - fight or flight: adrenaline from the adrenal glands into the blood → heart faster, blood diverted from gut and skin to muscles, faster breathing
   - endocrine system, with the glands of Fig 7.7
   - thyroxin, iodine and goitre
   - growth hormone (pituitary), dwarfism; the hypothalamus and releasing hormones
   - puberty: testosterone, oestrogen
   - insulin (pancreas), diabetes
   - feedback mechanism
   - Table 7.1 to complete
   - finale: comparing nervous and hormonal control; fair day

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-control)
- **neuronSignal**: a stepped impulse through a neuron. Stimulus at the dendrite → cell body → along the axon → chemicals released at the end → across the synapse → next neuron, or a muscle (neuromuscular junction). Parts labelled.
- **reflexArc**: a hand on a hot tawa. Choose the "reflex" path (spinal cord) or the "think first" path (up to the brain and back), then step it. A time counter shows the thinking path arriving too late (burnt). The signal still goes up to the brain on the reflex path.
- **brainMap**: the brain from the side (cerebrum/fore-brain, hypothalamus, pituitary, mid-brain, pons, cerebellum, medulla, spinal cord). Tap a part to see its job. In ask mode, an action (riding a bicycle, salivating, solving a sum, feeling full) asks the learner to tap the part in charge.
- **plantMovement**: kinds:
  - touch-me-not: tap a leaf, the message spreads, leaflets fold, then reopen
  - tendril coiling on a stick
  - phototropism: the light's side can be set, days grow, and an auxin overlay shows auxin on the shady side
  - geotropism: turn the pot, roots grow down, shoot up
  - hydrotropism: roots grow towards the water
  - pollen tube growing to the ovule
- **plantHormones**: a plant with the four hormones. Tap one to see where it acts and whether it promotes or stops growth.
- **endocrineMap**: a body outline (male/female toggle) with the glands of Fig 7.7. Tap a gland to see its hormone and job.
- **hormoneResponse**: two modes.
  - adrenaline: a scare → heart rate up, breathing up, blood to the muscles up, to the gut down; then calm.
  - blood sugar: eat → sugar rises → the pancreas releases insulin → sugar falls → insulin drops. A "no insulin" switch (diabetes) keeps the sugar high.
- **controlScene**: story illustrations: the science fair stall, the ruler drop, the hot tawa, the chhui-mui, the bean box, stage fright, iodised salt, fair day.
