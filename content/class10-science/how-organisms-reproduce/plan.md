# Class 10 Science, Ch8: How do Organisms Reproduce? (plan)

Source: NCERT jesc108 (2020-21, full edition). Keep everything, including the "More to Know" box on tissue culture, all eight activities, puberty, the human reproductive systems, menstruation and reproductive health (contraception, STDs, the sex-selection law, population).

Structure: theory-first, like Light L1. Each lesson runs Hook (story) → Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props) → See it in life (story, explained) → Practice (worked examples, NCERT questions and exercises, your turn, mission log). Hindi goes with every string.

## Mission: Green Sunday, 50 plants without buying one
The housing society announces Green Sunday: every family brings plants for a plant sale in three weeks. Grandpa promises fifty pots, and says he won't buy a single plant or seed: "My garden will make them."

Riya and Kabir have to work out how a living thing makes more of itself, and which way is best for each plant in the garden. They try several methods:
- money-plant cuttings
- the leaf buds of Grandpa's Bryophyllum (patharchatta)
- sprouting potato pieces
- a grafted rose
- saving seed from the pea flowers that the bees visit

Along the way:
- Mom's bread goes mouldy (spores).
- Kabir brings a jar of green pond water (Spirogyra).
- Riya grows yeast in sugar water.

Finale: the plant sale. Then Grandpa saves seeds from his tallest pea plants for next year: "Tall parents, tall children." That line leads straight into Ch9's pea mystery.

**Human reproduction is taught without the family story.** Lessons 6 and 7 use one neutral frame: the school's Class 10 health class, with a teacher at the board. They are taught at NCERT's own level and tone: factual, respectful and simple, with NCERT-style schematic labelled diagrams. There are no jokes and no story beats in those two lessons, and their questions are factual recall and reasoning from the text.

Roadmap stops:
1. Why make copies at all?
2. One parent, one cell
3. One parent, a piece of a plant
4. Why two parents?
5. Flowers, seeds and fruits
6. Growing up: the human body
7. Health and choices; the plant sale

## Lessons
1. **Do organisms make exact copies of themselves?** (8.1, 8.1.1)
   - Why reproduce, when it doesn't keep an individual alive.
   - Species are noticed because there are many alike.
   - Blueprints are DNA in the chromosomes; DNA is the information for making proteins.
   - Reproduction copies DNA and builds extra cellular apparatus, then the cell divides.
   - No copying reaction is perfect, so copies are similar but not identical; a drastic change makes a cell that dies.
   - Variation is the basis of evolution (a pointer to Ch9).
   - Importance of variation: niches, stability of populations, and heat-resistant bacteria surviving warming.
   - Prop: `dnaCopy` (copy mode, generations mode, niche mode).
   - Hook: Green Sunday and Grandpa's promise.
   - In life: why Grandpa's cuttings look exactly like the mother plant, and why a garden full of identical plants is a risk.
2. **Fission, fragmentation, regeneration, budding** (8.2.1–8.2.4)
   - Activity 8.1: yeast in sugar water budding. Activity 8.2: the bread mould is noticed, and its explanation is kept for lesson 3.
   - Modes depend on body design.
   - Binary fission: Amoeba in any plane (Activity 8.3); Leishmania (kala-azar) in a definite orientation.
   - Multiple fission: Plasmodium.
   - Yeast budding.
   - Fragmentation: Spirogyra (Activity 8.4); why many-celled organisms can't just split cell by cell.
   - A single cell type that can make other cell types.
   - Regeneration: Planaria and Hydra; development; regeneration is not reproduction.
   - Budding in Hydra.
   - Prop: `asexualModes` (stepped, one kind at a time).
   - Hook: Riya's yeast tube and Kabir's pond jar.
   - In life: what Riya sees under the school microscope.
3. **Vegetative propagation and spores** (8.2.5, 8.2.6, the tissue-culture box)
   - Roots, stems and leaves can make new plants.
   - Layering and grafting (sugarcane, rose, grape).
   - Advantages: earlier flowers and fruit, seedless plants (banana, orange, rose, jasmine), and all the plants are like the parent.
   - Activity 8.5: potato pieces with and without buds.
   - Bryophyllum leaf buds.
   - Activity 8.6: money-plant pieces with a leaf vs between leaves.
   - Tissue culture.
   - Rhizopus: hyphae vs sporangia, spores with thick walls.
   - Definition of asexual reproduction.
   - Props: `plantPropagation`, `asexualModes` (spores).
   - Hook: Grandpa's tray of potato pieces.
   - In life: which methods fill most of the fifty pots, and why the rose is grafted.
4. **Why the sexual mode?** (8.3, 8.3.1)
   - Bulls and calves, hens and chicks: two individuals.
   - DNA copying makes variation slowly; combining DNA from two individuals makes new combinations fast.
   - The problem: DNA would double every generation.
   - Germ cells with half the chromosomes and DNA (meiosis); fusion restores the number in the zygote.
   - Food store for the zygote: in simple organisms the germ cells are alike; in complex ones there is a big egg with food (the female gamete) and a small motile sperm (the male gamete).
   - Prop: `germCells` (doubling vs halving, gametes).
   - Hook: why Grandpa's peas need flowers and bees, but his money plant doesn't.
   - In life: seedlings vary, cuttings don't.
5. **Sexual reproduction in flowering plants** (8.3.2)
   - The parts of a flower: sepals, petals, stamens (anther, filament), pistil (stigma, style, ovary, ovule).
   - Unisexual (papaya, watermelon) and bisexual (Hibiscus, mustard) flowers.
   - Pollen, and the ovule's egg cell.
   - Self- vs cross-pollination; agents (wind, water, animals).
   - The pollen tube grows through the style; fertilisation forms the zygote.
   - Zygote → embryo; ovule → seed; ovary → fruit; the other parts shrivel.
   - Germination; Activity 8.7 (soaked chana: plumule, radicle, cotyledon).
   - Exercises 3 and 7.
   - Props: `flower`, `seedGermination`.
   - Hook: a bee on Grandpa's pea flowers.
   - In life: why Grandpa leaves the pea pods to dry on the plant.
6. **Reproduction in human beings: puberty and the reproductive systems** (8.3.3, a, b, the first part of the female system)
   - Neutral health-class frame, no family story.
   - Growth vs sexual maturation.
   - Changes common to boys and girls; changes that differ; changes are gradual and differ from person to person.
   - Why maturation comes when body growth slows: this is puberty.
   - Signals of maturity, and the need for special organs for the transfer of germ cells; the long pregnancy and breast-feeding in mammals.
   - Male system: testes in the scrotum (a lower temperature), testosterone, vas deferens, urethra, seminal vesicles and prostate (fluid and nutrition), sperm.
   - Female system: ovaries (eggs, hormones), one egg a month, oviduct (fallopian tube), uterus, cervix, vagina.
   - Fertilisation in the oviduct; zygote → embryo → implantation → foetus.
   - The uterus lining prepares every month; the placenta (villi, blood spaces, glucose and oxygen in, wastes out); about nine months; birth by rhythmic contractions of the uterus.
   - Exercises 2 and 5; in-text Q2, Q3 and Q4.
   - Prop: `humanSystems` (male, female, journey, placenta; tap an organ for its name and job).
7. **Menstruation and reproductive health** (8.3.3 c, d)
   - Neutral health-class frame for the learn part; the story returns only after the health content, on the plant-sale finale page.
   - When the egg isn't fertilised: it lives about a day, the lining breaks and leaves as blood and mucus, roughly monthly, for 2–8 days.
   - Maturation doesn't mean being ready; pressures from friends, family and government.
   - Sexually transmitted diseases: gonorrhoea and syphilis (bacterial), warts and HIV-AIDS (viral); a condom helps prevent many of them to some extent.
   - Pregnancy makes major demands on the body and mind; contraception categories:
     - barrier (condoms and similar coverings);
     - hormonal (oral pills, with side effects);
     - in the uterus (loop, copper-T; irritation);
     - surgical (blocking the vas deferens or fallopian tube).
   - Misuse: illegal sex-selective abortion of female foetuses; prenatal sex determination is prohibited by law; the child sex ratio.
   - Population size vs inequality.
   - In-text Q5 (copper-T and STDs); Exercises 6, 8 and 11.
   - Props: `menstrualCycle`, `contraceptionChart`.
   - Finale (a separate closing page after the health content): Green Sunday plant sale; all 7 stops recapped; Grandpa saves pea seeds, a teaser for Ch9.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-reproduction)
- **dnaCopy**:
  - copy mode: a cell's DNA strip is copied with one small change, apparatus is doubled, the cell divides;
  - generations mode: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 cells, small changes accumulate, and one drastic change gives a cell that dies;
  - niche mode: bacteria in a pond, the water warms, only the heat-resistant variants survive, then they multiply.
- **asexualModes**: stepped pictures of binary fission (Amoeba, Leishmania), multiple fission (Plasmodium), yeast budding, fragmentation (Spirogyra), regeneration (Planaria), budding (Hydra) and spores (Rhizopus: hyphae, sporangia, spores).
- **plantPropagation**: potato pieces (with and without buds, over days), Bryophyllum leaf buds, money-plant cuttings (node vs no node), layering, grafting (stock and scion) and tissue culture (tip → callus → plantlets → soil).
- **germCells**: doubling (if germ cells had full DNA, the amount doubles every generation) vs halving (germ cells carry half, and fusion restores it); gametes (similar in simple organisms, a big food-storing egg and a small motile sperm in complex ones).
- **flower**:
  - parts mode: a longitudinal section with tap-to-name parts and jobs;
  - unisexual / bisexual;
  - pollination mode: self vs cross, wind / water / insect;
  - fertilisation steps: pollen on stigma → tube through style → reaches ovule → fusion → zygote;
  - fruit steps: embryo, seed, fruit, other parts fall.
- **seedGermination**: soaked chana, step by step; plumule, radicle, cotyledons (Fig 8.9).
- **humanSystems**: NCERT-style schematic labelled diagrams, simple and non-graphic.
  - Male and female systems: tap an organ for its name and job.
  - Journey: egg from ovary → oviduct → fertilisation → embryo → implantation.
  - Placenta close-up: villi and blood spaces, what passes each way.
- **menstrualCycle**: a simple monthly cycle diagram: the lining thickens, the egg is released, if not fertilised it lives about a day, the lining breaks down (2–8 days), repeat.
- **contraceptionChart**: tap a method (condom, oral pills, copper-T / loop, surgical blocking) to see its category, how it stops fertilisation, side effects, and whether it helps against STDs.
