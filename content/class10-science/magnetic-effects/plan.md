# Class 10 Science, Ch13: Magnetic Effects of Electric Current (plan)

Source: NCERT jesc113 (2020-21, the full edition; keep everything: motor, induction, generator, domestic circuits, the Oersted, Faraday and "Magnetism in medicine" boxes). Structure: theory-first, like Light L1. Every lesson runs Hook (story) → Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props) → See it in life (story, explained) → Practice (examples, in-text questions and exercises, your turn, mission log). Hindi with every string.

## Mission: never again in the dark
Same family, a few weeks after the bill came down (Ch12). Another power cut, the third this month. Grandpa says his father had a bicycle lamp that lit itself "with no cell inside". Kabir wants to know how, and Riya wants a torch that never needs charging. Mission: build a hand-crank torch (a tiny generator) and, on the way, fix Grandpa's old mixer and make the house wiring safe.

Along the way:
- Kabir's compass goes crazy near the extension cord when the heater is on (Oersted);
- the kabadiwala's crane lifts a car with no hook (electromagnet);
- Grandpa's mixer is opened up (motor);
- Kabir's bicycle dynamo lights its lamp (induction);
- Mom's iron gives a tingle (earth wire);
- the finale: the next power cut, and the crank torch glows.

Roadmap of stops: (1) magnets and field lines, (2) the field of a current, (3) loops, solenoids and electromagnets, (4) the force on a current, (5) the electric motor, (6) electromagnetic induction, (7) the generator, (8) the wiring at home.

## Lessons
1. **Magnetic field and field lines (13.1, Activity 13.1–13.3).**
   - Hook: the power cut, the mission and roadmap; Kabir's compass jumps near the cord.
   - Learn: a compass is a small magnet; N-seeking and S-seeking poles; like poles repel; magnetic field; iron filings; drawing a field line with a compass; field lines leave N and enter S, are closed curves, are crowded where the field is strong, and never cross.
   - Practice: in-text Q (why the needle deflects), draw and list the properties, why lines don't cross.
2. **The field of a current in a straight wire (13.2, 13.2.1, 13.2.2, Activities 13.1, 13.4, 13.5, the Oersted box).**
   - Learn: concentric circles; the field reverses with the current; it grows with the current and weakens with distance; the right-hand thumb rule (Maxwell's corkscrew).
   - Practice: Example 13.1 (power line east to west), Exercises 1 and 15(i).
3. **Loop, solenoid, electromagnet (13.2.3–13.2.4, Activity 13.6).**
   - Learn: each part of the loop adds to the field inside; n turns give n times the field; a solenoid is like a bar magnet with a uniform field inside; the electromagnet.
   - Hook and in-life: the kabadiwala's crane.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–3 (clockwise loop on the table, uniform field, solenoid MCQ), Exercises 6(c), 7 and 8.
4. **Force on a current in a magnetic field (13.3, Activity 13.7).**
   - Learn: Ampère's idea; the rod jumps; reversing the current or the field reverses the force; the force is largest at right angles; Fleming's left-hand rule; electrons move opposite to the current; the "Magnetism in medicine" box (MRI).
   - Practice: Example 13.2, in-text Q1–3, Exercises 9 and 10.
5. **The electric motor (13.4).**
   - Learn: the coil ABCD, the split ring, the brushes; the forces on AB and CD; the half-turn reversal (commutator); armature, electromagnet and many turns in commercial motors.
   - Hook and in-life: Grandpa's mixer.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–3, Exercises 6(a), 11 and 12.
6. **Electromagnetic induction (13.5, Activities 13.8–13.9, the Faraday box).**
   - Learn: a magnet moved in or out kicks the galvanometer, and holding it still gives nothing; moving the coil instead does the same; switching the primary on or off induces current in the secondary; primary and secondary coils; Fleming's right-hand rule.
   - Hook and in-life: the bicycle dynamo.
   - Practice: in-text Q, Exercises 2, 13, 14 and 15(iii).
7. **The electric generator (13.6).**
   - Learn: the rotating coil, slip rings, brushes; the current reverses every half turn, giving AC; a split ring gives DC; 50 Hz in India; why AC suits long distances.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–4, Exercises 3, 4, 6(b) and 16.
   - In-life: the crank torch is built.
8. **Domestic electric circuits (13.7).**
   - Learn: live (red), neutral (black), earth (green), 220 V; meter, main fuse, main switch; 15 A and 5 A circuits; appliances in parallel; the earth wire on metal bodies; overloading and short-circuit; the fuse.
   - Hook: Mom's iron tingles.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–3 (the 2 kW oven on a 5 A circuit), Exercises 5, 6(d), 17 and 18.
   - Finale: the next power cut, the crank torch lights, all eight stops recapped.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-magnetism)
- **magneticField:** a bar magnet, a straight wire seen from above (⊙/⊗), a loop or a solenoid seen in cross-section. Field lines traced from the real field, iron-filing dashes, arrows, and a compass the learner taps anywhere to place; the needle follows the field. Current can be set and reversed; the magnet can be flipped. Derived: the field's direction and relative strength at the compass.
- **handRule:** the right-hand thumb rule, Fleming's left-hand rule and Fleming's right-hand rule. The learner sets two directions (up, down, left, right, out of the page, into the page) and the third follows. Shows the three fingers as perpendicular arrows. Handles electrons and positive particles (Example 13.2, in-text Q3, Exercise 10).
- **forceOnRod:** Activity 13.7. The rod hangs between the poles of a horseshoe magnet, seen end-on. Current B→A or A→B and its size, flip the magnet, a stronger magnet, a longer rod; the rod swings left or right by the force.
- **motor:** the simple motor seen end-on. Coil arms AB (⊗/⊙), field arrows, the forces on each arm, the split ring and brushes X and Y. Turn the coil step by step to see the current reverse at the half turn, or run it. Switch the split ring for plain rings and the coil only rocks.
- **induction:** Activity 13.8 (move the magnet or the coil in, out, or hold it; N or S facing; the galvanometer kicks right or left, then returns to zero) and Activity 13.9 (primary with a key, secondary with a galvanometer: kicks on switching on and off only).
- **generator:** the rotating coil end-on with the induced current in each arm. Slip rings (AC) or a split ring (DC), with a graph of the output current against the angle over two turns and a marker at the coil's angle. Step or crank.
- **homeWiring:** Fig 13.20 as an interactive schematic. Mains live, neutral and earth; meter, main fuse, switch; a 5 A circuit (bulb, fan, TV) and a 15 A circuit (geyser, cooler, iron), each with appliances in parallel. Switch appliances on and read the currents. Plug the 2 kW oven into the 5 A circuit. Make a short circuit or a leak to the iron's metal body, with or without the earth wire; the fuse melts, or the body becomes live.
- **magnetismScene:** story panels in the shared Person look: PowerCutAgain, CompassJumps, ScrapCrane, MixerOpen, BicycleDynamo, IronTingle, MeterBoard, CrankTorch.
