## Mission: the case of the shrinking bangles
This is NCERT Exercise 15 told as a mystery. It's the same family as Ch1 (Riya, Kabir, Mom, Grandpa).
- Mom's old gold bangles, Grandma's, have gone dull.
- A man at the door calls himself a goldsmith and offers to make them shine. He dips them in a liquid; they sparkle like new, and he leaves quickly.
- On the kitchen scale they now weigh much less.
- Riya and Kabir open a case file. Every lesson gives them one clue:
  - what gold is, and how to tell a real metal;
  - why gold never tarnishes;
  - which liquid can dissolve gold at all;
  - where gold comes from and how it is purified;
  - why jewellery is 22 carat.
- The finale names the solution (aqua regia), explains the lost weight, and warns the neighbours: buy hallmarked gold from a real jeweller.
- Side errands from the family's life tie in: cooking pans, the school bell, the rusty bicycle, the iron pillar at the Qutub Minar on a school trip, and a railway track being welded.

Each lesson ends with **Case notes** (clues found, questions still open), in place of a mission log.

## Lessons
1. **Metals and non-metals: physical properties**
   - Hook (full opener): the family, the goldsmith, the lighter bangles, the case file and the roadmap of clues.
   - Learn: element and metal/non-metal recap (Class IX), then Activities 3.1–3.7:
     - metallic lustre (sandpaper)
     - hardness (knife; sodium cuts)
     - malleability (hammer; gold and silver the most malleable)
     - ductility (2 km of wire from 1 g of gold)
     - heat conduction (wax pin; silver and copper best, lead and mercury poor)
     - electricity (bulb; why wires are coated in PVC)
     - sonority (school bells)
   - Learn: non-metals (C, S, I, O, H; bromine the only liquid) and Table 3.1. Physical properties alone can't classify, so the exceptions:
     - mercury is liquid; gallium and caesium melt in the palm
     - iodine is lustrous
     - carbon's allotropes: diamond is the hardest, graphite conducts
     - alkali metals are soft
   - In life: is the bangle still a metal? (lustre, malleable, rings).
2. **Metals and oxygen**
   - Activity 3.8: MgO ash dissolves to a basic solution; burnt sulphur gives an acidic one (litmus). Metal oxides are basic, non-metal oxides acidic.
   - Metal + oxygen → metal oxide (2Cu + O₂, 4Al + 3O₂). Amphoteric oxides: Al₂O₃ with HCl and with NaOH; ZnO.
   - Soluble oxides give alkalis (Na₂O, K₂O).
   - Activity 3.9, different reactivities:
     - K and Na catch fire, so they are kept in kerosene;
     - Mg, Al, Zn and Pb get a protective oxide layer;
     - iron filings burn in a flame;
     - copper gets a black coat;
     - silver and gold don't react at all.
   - Anodising.
   - In life: the bangles never tarnished, so gold sits at the bottom.
3. **Metals with water and acids**
   - Activity 3.10: K and Na react violently and the H₂ catches fire; Ca floats; Mg reacts with hot water; Al, Fe and Zn only with steam; Pb, Cu, Ag and Au not at all. Equations.
   - Activity 3.11 (dilute HCl, thermometers): Mg > Al > Zn > Fe; Cu no reaction.
   - Nitric acid gives no H₂ (except Mg and Mn with very dilute HNO₃).
   - Aqua regia, 3 : 1 conc HCl : conc HNO₃, dissolves gold and platinum. This is the big clue.
   - In life: what the "goldsmith's" liquid must have been.
4. **The reactivity series**
   - Activity 3.12: Fe in CuSO₄ vs Cu in FeSO₄. Metal A + salt of B → salt of A + B.
   - Table 3.2: K … Au, with [H].
   - NCERT's A/B/C/D table puzzle; Zn in FeSO₄; Exercise 1.
   - In life: Kabir's test-tube tournament of the family's metals.
5. **How metals and non-metals react: ionic compounds**
   - Noble gases and a filled valence shell (Table 3.3), shell recap.
   - Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ and Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻, giving NaCl (Fig 3.5); MgCl₂ (Fig 3.6).
   - Cation and anion; ionic (electrovalent) compounds; electron-dot structures.
   - Na₂O and MgO (in-text Q1).
   - In life: table salt in the kitchen is ions, not molecules.
6. **Properties of ionic compounds**
   - Activity 3.13: solid and hard but brittle; high melting and boiling points (Table 3.4); soluble in water, not in kerosene or petrol.
   - They conduct when dissolved or molten, not when solid.
   - In life: why salt water conducts (wet hands near switches).
7. **Getting metals out of the ground**
   - Earth's crust and seawater; minerals, ores, gangue.
   - Fig 3.9 (series → method) and Fig 3.10 (steps).
   - Enrichment.
   - Low reactivity metals (HgS, Cu₂S, by heating alone); gold and silver are found free (native).
   - Middle metals: roasting (ZnS) vs calcination (ZnCO₃), then reduction with carbon (ZnO + C).
   - Displacement as reduction: 3MnO₂ + 4Al; the thermit reaction joining railway tracks.
   - In life: why the bangle's gold was "found free", and the railway weld.
8. **The most reactive metals, and refining**
   - Electrolysis of molten chlorides (Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na at the cathode, 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ at the anode); Al from Al₂O₃.
   - Electrolytic refining (Fig 3.12): the impure anode, pure cathode and salt solution; anode mud.
   - In life: gold is refined this way; Exercise 8.
9. **Corrosion, and alloys**
   - Recap: silver goes black (Ag₂S), copper goes green (basic copper carbonate), iron rusts.
   - Activity 3.14, tubes A/B/C: air and water are both needed.
   - Prevention:
     - painting, oiling, greasing
     - galvanising: still safe when scratched, because zinc is more reactive
     - chrome plating, anodising
     - alloys
   - Alloys: steel (C); stainless steel (Ni, Cr); amalgam; brass, bronze and solder conduct less and melt lower; 24 vs 22 carat gold.
   - The iron pillar.
   - Exercises 2, 4 (tin vs zinc cans), 12, 13 (lemon on copper vessels), 15, 16.
   - **Finale**: the case closed, with every clue recapped.

## Props (in @chalk/prop-chemistry, new files)
- **propertyTester**: pick a sample (Fe, Cu, Al, Mg, Na, Zn, Pb, Au, coal, graphite, diamond, S, I₂) and a test (sandpaper, knife, hammer, wire, heat with wax pin, bulb circuit, strike). The result is animated with words, and a results table (Table 3.1) builds up as samples are tried. The exceptions come out honestly: sodium cuts, graphite conducts, iodine shines, diamond scratches the knife.
- **reactivityLab**: a metal (K … Au) in a reagent: heat in air, cold water, hot water, steam, dilute HCl, or a salt solution (NaCl, MgCl₂, ZnSO₄, FeSO₄, CuSO₄, AgNO₃).
  - Shows bubbles at the right rate, flames, floating, colour change, a metal coating and the thermometer.
  - Has an optional series ladder that highlights the metal.
  - Lessons can restrict which metals and reagents appear.
- **ionicBond**: NaCl, KCl, MgCl₂, Na₂O, MgO, stepped: atoms with K/L/M shells → electrons move → ions with charges and configurations → held together. An electron-dot (valence only) option.
- **ionicTester**: NaCl, CaCl₂ or MgCl₂ as a solid, in water, in kerosene, or molten. A bulb circuit lights only when the ions are free; the particle view shows a locked lattice vs moving ions.
- **extractionFlow**: pick a metal (Au, Ag, Hg, Cu, Zn, Na, Mg, Al) and, for zinc, the ore (sulphide or carbonate). It steps along Fig 3.10's path with each equation, beside Fig 3.9's series bands.
- **thermitWeld**: two rail ends; Fe₂O₃ + Al in a crucible; molten iron pours in and joins them.
- **refiningCell**: electrolytic refining of copper. The anode thins, the cathode thickens, Cu²⁺ ions move, and anode mud collects.
- **rustLab**: Activity 3.14 (tubes A/B/C, wait days), and a protect mode with a coating (none, paint, grease, zinc, tin, chrome, stainless steel) plus scratch and wait.
- **goldCarat**: 24/22/18/14 carat as 24 parts gold vs copper/silver, with a hardness bar.
- **metalScene**: story pictures:
  - Goldsmith at the door
  - Lighter bangles on the kitchen scale
  - Case file desk
  - Kitchen pans
  - Iron pillar
  - Railway weld
  - Hallmarked jeweller
  - Case closed
