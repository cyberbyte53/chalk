# Class 10 Science, Ch16: Sustainable Management of Natural Resources (plan)

**Source.** NCERT jesc116 (2020-21, full edition). Keep everything: the Ganga Action Plan and Namami Gange box, coliform (Fig 16.1), Activity 16.3 (pH of water), the five R's, sustainable development, the Atharva Veda hymns and sacred groves, stakeholders, Bishnoi/Amrita Devi, the Great Himalayan National Park, Chipko, Arabari, kulhs, dams (Indira Gandhi Canal, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri, Tawa), water harvesting (khadins and the list of regional systems, check dams, Rajendra Singh), coal and petroleum, choices, Euro norms, and the 16.5 overview.

**Structure.** Theory-first, like Light L1:
1. Hook (story).
2. Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props).
3. See it in life (story, explained).
4. Practice (NCERT questions, exercises, your turn, mission log).

Hindi with every string. Many exercises are open-ended ("what changes would you make…"); turn them into choose-the-best-action and sort-the-actions questions, with no single-word recall of opinions.

## Mission: The Village Audit (the science book's finale)
After the Bottle Pond, the eco-club sets its final project of the year: a **resource audit** of one real place. Riya and Kabir go with Grandpa to his home village for the summer, his first visit in twenty years (Activity 16.4: new roads and houses). The village has these troubles:
- The old well dries up by March.
- The grove behind the temple is half cut.
- The canal from the big dam never reaches the farms at the tail end.
- The river ghat is dirty.
- Everyone rides scooters to the market 2 km away.

**Audit sheet:** one row per resource (water, forest, river, fuel, our own habits), each with what's wrong and what would fix it. Each lesson fills one row.

**Finale: the gram sabha.** The kids present the audit. A check dam is planned with the villagers, the grove gets a village committee (the Arabari idea), the family's week changes, and Kabir closes the whole science book: "Everything we learnt this year is the same earth, one family."

**Roadmap stops:**
1. one earth, one family (pollution, coliform, the five R's)
2. why manage at all (limited, fair shares, waste)
3. who wants the forest (stakeholders)
4. people who saved forests (Bishnoi, Chipko, Arabari)
5. water for all (kulhs, big dams)
6. catch every drop (water harvesting)
7. fuel that runs out (coal, petroleum, choices), then the finale

## Lessons
1. **One earth, one family** (opening section to Activity 16.4)
   - Vasudhaiv kutumbakam; laws and organisations.
   - The Ganga Action Plan (1985); coliform defined; the pollution box; Namami Gange.
   - Measurable factors and pH (Activity 16.3).
   - The five R's, each defined; sustainable development.
   - Props: riverHealth (pollution sources and coliform; pH test), fiveRs sorter.
   - In life: the dirty ghat; the family's first week of the five R's.
   - Practice: in-text Q1, Exercises 1, 5 and 6.
2. **Why manage our resources?** (16.1, Activity 16.5, the hymns, sacred groves)
   - Everything comes from the earth except sunlight; resources aren't unlimited and population is rising.
   - Long-term vs short-term; equitable distribution.
   - Damage while extracting (mining slag).
   - Traditional conservation.
   - Props: fossilClock in "share" mode is not needed; use resourcesScene and fiveRs (reduce focus).
   - In life: new roads and houses in the village (Activity 16.4) and where the materials came from.
   - Practice: in-text Q2–Q4.
3. **Forests and their stakeholders** (16.2, 16.2.1)
   - Biodiversity hotspot (number and range of species); loss of diversity and loss of stability.
   - The four stakeholders and what each needs or gets; monoculture plantations; industry moving on.
   - Bishnois and Amrita Devi (1731, 363 people, khejri).
   - The Great Himalayan National Park grazing example; tourists and development projects.
   - Prop: stakeholders (tap each).
   - In life: who wants the village grove.
   - Practice: Exercise 3.
4. **Managing forests with people** (16.2.2, Activity 16.8)
   - Chipko (Reni, early 1970s; lopping vs felling).
   - Arabari (1972, A.K. Banerjee, 1,272 ha, 25% of the harvest, Rs 12.5 crores by 1983).
   - Prop: stakeholders (case-study mode, stepped).
   - In life: a grove committee for the village.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q2 (forests).
5. **Water for all** (16.3, kulhs, 16.3.1)
   - Monsoon rain is concentrated; loss of vegetation, water-hungry crops, pollution.
   - Local irrigation and the British mega-projects.
   - Kulhs (farthest village first).
   - Dams: what they give (storage, irrigation, electricity; the Indira Gandhi Canal); the three problems (social, economic, environmental); Narmada Bachao Andolan, Tehri, the Tawa oustees.
   - Prop: waterHarvest (kulh mode; dam trade-offs).
   - In life: the tail-end farms.
6. **Catch every drop** (16.3.2, Fig 16.4)
   - Watershed management; the regional systems list (khadins, tanks and nadis, bandharas and tals, bundhis, ahars and pynes, kulhs, eris, surangams, kattas).
   - Check dams; why groundwater is better (no evaporation, spreads to wells, no mosquitoes, protected from waste).
   - Rajendra Singh's johads.
   - Prop: waterHarvest (khadin and check-dam steps; compare surface pond vs groundwater).
   - In life: the check dam for the dry well.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q3 (water).
7. **Coal and petroleum** (16.4, 16.5)
   - Formed from biomass, so they will run out (petroleum about 40 years, coal about 200 years at the present rate).
   - Combustion products (CO₂, H₂O, oxides of N and S; CO in insufficient air); greenhouse gas and global warming.
   - Choices: bus/vehicle/walk, LED/tube, lift/stairs, sweater/heater; efficient engines and Euro norms.
   - The overview.
   - Prop: fossilClock (last, burn, choices modes).
   - In life: the scooter to the market.
   - Practice: Exercises 2, 4 and 7.
   - Finale: the gram sabha, all 7 stops, and the close of the science book.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-resources)
- **fiveRs**: sort everyday actions into Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Repurpose and Recycle. Shows NCERT's definitions; counts right and wrong.
- **riverHealth**:
  - River mode: a river past a town; toggle the pollution sources (untreated sewage, garbage, bathing and washing, ashes, industrial effluents) and the treatment plant. Shows coliform as low, high or very high, and fish alive or dying. This is qualitative: no invented counts.
  - pH mode (Activity 16.3): test water samples with universal indicator; the samples are set by the lesson.
- **stakeholders**:
  - Map mode: forest in the middle with the four stakeholders (local people, Forest Department, industrialists, wildlife and nature enthusiasts). Tap one to see what they need or get, how dependent they are, and their stake in one forest's future.
  - Cases mode: stepped stories of Bishnoi/Amrita Devi, the Himalayan park meadows, Chipko and Arabari.
- **waterHarvest**:
  - Kulh mode: water reaches the farthest village first.
  - Khadin mode: monsoon rain fills behind the embankment, then soaks into the ground, then the well rises, then a crop grows on the moist bed.
  - Compare mode: a big dam vs local harvesting, on who benefits, the three problems, evaporation, mosquitoes and recharge.
- **fossilClock**:
  - Last mode: years left at the present rate (petroleum 40, coal 200, from NCERT) and a "use less" slider that stretches them (years = reserve ÷ rate).
  - Burn mode: C, H, N, S in → CO₂, H₂O, NOx, SOx out; insufficient air gives CO.
  - Choices mode: the four pairs, pick the lower-fuel choice.
- **resourcesScene**: story panels:

  - khejri
  - chipko
  - tailEnd
  - checkDam
