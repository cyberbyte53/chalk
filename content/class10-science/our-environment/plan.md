3. See it in life (story, explained).

## Mission: The Bottle Pond
The school eco-club's challenge for Environment Week has two halves.

**1. Keep a sealed jar of pond life alive for 30 days.** Riya and Kabir scoop water, weed, snails and a few tiny fish from Grandpa's terrace tub-pond. Their first jar goes cloudy and smelly: too many fish, no plants, kept in the dark. Each lesson fixes one thing:
- what a jar needs (an ecosystem with producers, consumers and decomposers, plus sunlight);
- who eats whom (food chains);
- why one plant can't feed ten fish (energy flow, the 10% law).

Along the way Grandpa refuses to spray his vegetables, which brings in biological magnification, and an old fridge going for scrap brings in the ozone layer.

**2. Halve the family's garbage for the week.**
- Activity 15.5's burial pit in Grandpa's garden shows what rots and what doesn't.
- The kulhad story from a train trip explains disposable cups.
- Segregating the household waste closes the week.

**Finale: Environment Day.** The jar is alive on day 30, the family's bin is half as full, and Kabir explains every stop to the eco-club.

**Roadmap stops:**
1. what a jar-world needs (ecosystem)
2. who eats whom (chains, webs, trophic levels)
3. where the energy goes (10% law)
4. poison that climbs (biological magnification)
5. the sky's sunscreen (ozone)
6. what rots and what stays (biodegradable or not)
7. our garbage (managing waste), then the finale

## Lessons
1. **What is an ecosystem?** (15.1, Activities 15.1–15.2)
   - Environment; biotic vs abiotic components.
   - Natural vs human-made ecosystems (garden, forest, pond, lake; garden, crop field, aquarium).
   - Designing an aquarium; producers (green plants and certain bacteria, photosynthesis), consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores, parasites), decomposers (bacteria and fungi; what happens without them).
   - Why an aquarium needs cleaning but a pond doesn't.
   - Prop: ecosystem (jar builder).
   - In life: why the first jar went cloudy.
2. **Food chains, trophic levels and food webs.** (15.1.1, first part)
   - Food chain; trophic level; first to fourth levels (producers; herbivores or primary consumers; small carnivores or secondary consumers; large carnivores or tertiary consumers).
   - Fig 15.1 chains (forest, grassland, pond), Fig 15.2 levels, and the food web (Fig 15.3).
   - Prop: foodChain (chain, web).
   - In life: Kabir's "who eats whom" chain for the jar; why no big fish in the jar.
3. **Where the energy goes.** (15.1.1, second part, Fig 15.4)
   - Plants capture about 1% of sunlight; heat lost at each step; about 10% passes to the next level.
   - Why chains have only 3–4 steps; most individuals at the lowest level; energy flow is one-way.
   - Prop: foodChain (pyramid mode, energy numbers).
   - In life: one weed, ten fish.
   - Practice: Exercises 4 and 5 (removing a trophic level).
4. **Biological magnification.** (Activity 15.3)
   - Pesticides washed into soil and water, taken up by plants; not degradable, so they build up at each level; humans at the top get the most.
   - Residues in grains, fruit and meat can't always be washed off.
   - Prop: bioMagnification.
   - In life: why Grandpa won't spray.
   - Practice: Exercise 6.
5. **The ozone layer.** (15.2.1, Activity 15.4)
   - O₃ vs O₂: a deadly poison at ground level, but a UV shield up high; UV causes skin cancer.
   - Formation: UV splits O₂ into O + O, then O + O₂ gives O₃.
   - The 1980s drop; CFCs (refrigerants, fire extinguishers); UNEP 1987 agreement; CFC-free fridges.
   - Prop: ozoneLayer (formation steps, CFC shield).
   - In life: the old fridge going for scrap.
   - Practice: Exercise 9.
6. **Biodegradable or not.** (15.2.2, Activities 15.5–15.6)
   - Waste materials; the burial activity at 15-day checks.
   - Enzymes are specific (why we can't eat coal), so plastics persist.
   - Definitions: biodegradable vs non-biodegradable, and how each affects the environment.
   - Prop: wasteBurial.
   - In life: Grandpa's pit, dug up on day 60.
   - Practice: Exercise 1 and the in-text questions.
7. **Managing our garbage.** (Activities 15.7–15.9, "Think it over")
   - Lifestyle and packaging changes; collection and segregation; local bodies; sewage and industry waste.
   - Disposable cups: returnable plastic glass, then disposable plastic, then kulhads (loss of topsoil), then paper cups.
   - E-waste; recycling plastics; environment-friendly practices.
   - Props: wasteSorter, cupChoice.
   - In life: the family's segregated week.
   - Practice: Exercises 3, 7 and 8.
   - Finale: Environment Day, all 7 stops.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-environment)
- **ecosystem**: a jar-world builder. Toggle sunlight, water plants (producers), snails and small fish (consumers, with fish count), decomposers (bacteria and fungi), and an air pump.
  - Derived: biotic and abiotic parts present, roles, whether the jar sustains itself, and what goes wrong (no producers, too many fish, no light, no decomposers so dead remains pile up).
- **foodChain**: Fig 15.1 chains (forest, grassland, pond).
  - Chain mode: reveal each trophic level with its name and consumer type.
  - Web mode: a small food web (Fig 15.3 style); tap an organism to see who eats it.
  - Pyramid mode: energy at each level from 10,000 kJ, ×10% each step.
  - Remove a level: the effect on the levels above and below.
- **bioMagnification**: pesticide sprayed on a crop or washed into a pond. Step up the chain; dots of chemical per individual multiply at each level, and the top level (humans) holds the most.
- **ozoneLayer**:
  - Formation mode: stepped, UV splitting O₂ then O + O₂ giving O₃.
  - Shield mode: a CFC slider thins the layer and lets UV reach the ground.
  - The 1987 agreement brings CFCs to zero.
- **wasteBurial**: Activity 15.5 burial pit, with items buried under 15 cm of soil. "Wait 15 days" changes the biodegradable items; plastics, glass and foil stay unchanged. Peels and leaves change fastest.
- **wasteSorter**: household waste into wet (biodegradable) and dry (non-biodegradable) bins; counts right and wrong.
- **cupChoice**: the four train tea cups compared on reuse, biodegradable, topsoil and hygiene.
