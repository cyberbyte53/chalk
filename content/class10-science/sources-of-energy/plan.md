# Class 10 Science, Ch14: Sources of Energy (plan)

Source: NCERT jesc114 (2020-21, the full edition). Keep everything, including the Do You Know boxes (solar constant, Denmark's winds, E = Δmc², nuclear fusion) and the Think-it-over boxes.

Structure is theory-first, like Light L1. Every lesson runs:
- Hook (story)
- Learn (NCERT order, define before use, props)
- See it in life (story, explained)
- Practice (in-text questions and exercises, your turn, mission log)

Hindi with every string.

## Mission: lights for Grandpa's village
It's the same family, after the bill (Ch12) and the crank torch (Ch13). In the summer holidays they visit Grandpa's village in the hills. The lights come for a few hours a day. The school's evening classes run by a smoky kerosene lamp. Kitchens fill with smoke from wood and cow-dung cakes. The sarpanch asks the visiting "city kids" to make an energy plan for the village and present it at the panchayat meeting on the last day.

Riya keeps the plan's notebook. Kabir checks every idea against the four questions of a good source:
- How much work does it do per kilogram?
- Is it easy to get?
- Is it easy to store and carry?
- Is it cheap?

Grandpa has been here sixty years and remembers how things used to be (Activity 14.4).

Roadmap of stops:
1. What makes a good source.
2. Fossil fuels and the thermal power plant.
3. Hydro power.
4. Biomass and biogas.
5. Wind.
6. The Sun.
7. Sea and earth's heat.
8. Nuclear energy.
9. The environment, how long sources last, and the plan.

## Lessons

### 1. What is a good source of energy? (opener, 14.1, Activities 14.1–14.2)
- **Hook:** the arrival, the dark village, the smoky chulha. The mission and the roadmap.
- **Learn:**
  - The riddle: energy is conserved, so why a crisis? Usable energy spreads out into less usable forms (the candle; the 75 °C water cooling to 25 °C).
  - A source of energy, and a fuel.
  - The four qualities of a good source.
  - Choosing a fuel depends on where you live and the job (Activity 14.2: forest, mountain village, Delhi, five centuries ago).
- **Props:** fuelCompare (heat per gram, smoke, availability, storage, cost).
- **See it in life:** why the village burns wood, and what's wrong with it.
- **Practice:** in-text Q1–3, Exercise 8.

### 2. Fossil fuels and thermal power (14.2.1–14.2.2, Activity 14.3, Fig 14.1)
- **Hook:** the high-tension line on the hill, and where the electricity is made.
- **Learn:**
  - Fossil fuels: formed over millions of years, limited reserves, non-renewable.
  - Pollution: acidic oxides of C, N and S, acid rain, the greenhouse effect.
  - Cutting pollution: efficient combustion, fewer escaping gases.
  - The turbine: Activity 14.3's ping-pong-ball model with dynamo and bulb.
  - The thermal power plant: fuel → heat → steam → turbine → generator. Plants sit near coal fields because transmission beats carrying coal.
- **Props:** powerPlant (mode model, then thermal).
- **See it in life:** the village line comes from a coal plant far away.
- **Practice:** in-text Q1–2 (disadvantages of fossil fuels, why alternatives), Exercise 4.

### 3. Hydro power (14.2.3)
- **Hook:** a trip to the dam upstream, and a submerged temple top in the reservoir.
- **Learn:**
  - Kinetic energy of flowing water becomes potential energy behind the dam, then kinetic energy through pipes to the turbine.
  - Renewable, because rain refills it.
  - A quarter of India's needs.
  - The problems: few sites, submerged farms and homes, destroyed ecosystems, methane from rotting plants, rehabilitation (Tehri, Sardar Sarovar).
- **Props:** powerPlant (hydro, with water level).
- **See it in life:** the submerged temple explained. Could the village have its own small dam?
- **Practice:** in-text Q3 (traditional water and wind energy modified), Exercise 5.

### 4. Biomass and biogas (14.2.4: bio-mass, charcoal, gobar-gas)
- **Hook:** Grandpa's neighbour's eight cows, and the dung-cake wall.
- **Learn:**
  - Biomass: wood, dung cakes, crop residue. Low heat, lots of smoke.
  - Charcoal: wood burnt with little oxygen, smokeless and hotter.
  - The biogas plant (Fig 14.4): slurry → digester → anaerobic micro-organisms → methane, CO₂, H₂, H₂S. Up to 75% methane. Stored in the gas tank. The slurry makes manure rich in N and P.
  - Is biomass renewable?
- **Props:** biogasPlant (stepped), fuelCompare (wood, cow-dung cake, charcoal, biogas).
- **See it in life:** plan item 1, a gobar-gas plant for the neighbour's kitchen.
- **Practice:** Exercise 2 and the bio-mass questions.

### 5. Wind energy (14.2.4: wind)
- **Hook:** the windy ridge above the village; Grandpa's old water-lifting windmill.
- **Learn:**
  - Unequal heating makes wind.
  - The windmill turns a generator.
  - One mill is small, so a wind energy farm couples many.
  - Limits: wind most of the year, speed over 15 km/h, back-up storage, about 2 hectares per MW, high cost and maintenance.
  - Do You Know: Denmark over 25%, India fifth, 45,000 MW potential, Kanyakumari 380 MW.
- **Props:** windFarm (wind speed, number of mills, battery back-up, land).
- **See it in life:** is the ridge windy enough all year?
- **Practice:** Exercise 6(a), the wind questions.

### 6. Solar energy (14.3.1, Activities 14.5–14.6)
- **Hook:** the hot flat roof of the school.
- **Learn:**
  - The Sun: 5 billion years. India receives 5,000 trillion kWh a year. The solar constant is 1.4 kW/m².
  - Black absorbs more heat (Activity 14.5's two flasks).
  - The solar cooker: black inside, a glass cover (greenhouse effect), a plane mirror (in-text Q1). Useful only at some times of day.
  - The solar cell: 0.5–1 V, about 0.7 W. Panels have no moving parts and suit remote places. Silicon and silver make them costly. Uses: satellites, traffic lights, calculators.
- **Props:** solarLab (flasks, cooker, panel).
- **See it in life:** plan item 2, solar panels for the school's evening lights, and a solar cooker for the mid-day meal.
- **Practice:** in-text Q1, Exercises 1 and 9.

### 7. Energy from the sea, and geothermal (14.3.2–14.3.3)
- **Hook:** cousin Meera calls from a coastal town; Kabir saw a hot spring on the trek (Manikaran-style).
- **Learn:**
  - Tidal energy: the Moon's pull, a dam across a narrow opening, a turbine.
  - Wave energy.
  - Ocean thermal energy: the surface is warm and the deep is cold. It needs a 20 °C (20 K) difference down to 2 km. Ammonia boils, turns the turbine, and is condensed by cold water.
  - The limits.
  - Geothermal energy: hot spots, steam, hot springs; few viable sites.
- **Props:** oceanEnergy (tidal, wave, OTEC), powerPlant (geothermal).
- **See it in life:** why none of these suits the hill village.
- **Practice:** in-text Q2–3, Exercises 6(b–c).

### 8. Nuclear energy (14.3.4 and the boxes)
- **Hook:** a news report on Tarapur on Grandpa's radio.
- **Learn:**
  - Nucleus; fission of uranium by a low-energy neutron gives lighter nuclei, neutrons and energy.
  - 10 million times the energy of burning one carbon atom.
  - The chain reaction, controlled in a reactor.
  - E = Δmc²; 1 u ≈ 931 MeV; 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
  - Indian plants (under 3%).
  - Hazards: waste, leaks, cost, limited uranium.
  - Fusion (²H + ²H → ³He + n) powers the Sun and needs extreme conditions.
- **Props:** fission (chain reaction, controlled or not; the mass-to-energy calculator).
- **See it in life:** not for the village, and why.
- **Practice:** in-text Q4, Exercise 3.

### 9. The environment, and how long sources last (14.4–14.5, Activities 14.7–14.9); finale
- **Learn:**
  - Every source disturbs the environment. "Cleaner", not "clean" (CNG; the solar cell's manufacture).
  - Exhaustible/non-renewable vs renewable/inexhaustible.
  - Coal for 200 more years, the Sun for 5 billion.
  - Most sources are stored solar energy; geothermal and nuclear are not.
- **Props:** energySorter (renewable or not; from the Sun or not).
- **See it in life:** the panchayat meeting, where Riya presents the plan: biogas, school solar panels, a solar cooker, charcoal or improved chulhas, no new dam, and save energy.
- **Practice:** in-text Qs (pollution-free? hydrogen vs CNG; two renewable, two exhaustible), Exercises 7 and 10.
- **Finale:** the school's first evening class under solar lights, recapping all 9 stops.

## Props (new package @chalk/prop-energy)
- **fuelCompare:** fuel cards and a bar chart comparing heat per gram (kJ/g, the Class 8 values), smoke, how easy it is to get, store and carry, and cost. Tap a fuel; derived: how many of the four "good source" checks it passes.
- **powerPlant:** the energy chain stepped through, from source → … → turbine → generator → wires.
  - Kinds: model (Activity 14.3 jet, ping-pong turbine, dynamo, bulb), thermal, hydro, geothermal, nuclear.
  - Hydro has a water-level setting, and output grows with height.
  - Derived: the energy forms in order, and whether it is renewable.
- **biogasPlant:** Fig 14.4. Mixing tank → digester (no oxygen) → days pass → gas collects in the dome → gas to the stove; slurry out as manure. Derived: gas made, the methane share.
- **windFarm:** wind speed in km/h, how many mills, battery back-up on or off. The blades turn above 15 km/h. Derived: is it turning, the total output in MW, the land needed (2 ha per MW), and power when the wind drops.
- **solarLab:**
  - Flasks mode: black vs white over time (Activity 14.5).
  - Cooker mode: black box, glass lid, mirror, each toggleable; temperature after an hour; can it cook?
  - Panel mode: number of cells, sunny/cloudy/night; volts and watts.
- **oceanEnergy:** tidal (high or low tide, water flows through the dam's turbine), wave, and OTEC (surface and deep temperatures; works if the difference is 20 °C or more).
- **fission:** a stepped chain reaction. A neutron hits U-235, which splits into two nuclei plus 2–3 neutrons; generations 0–3. Controlled (one neutron goes on) or uncontrolled (all go on). A Δm-to-energy readout (931 MeV per u).
- **energySorter:** tap each source and put it in a bin. Two sorts: renewable vs non-renewable, and from the Sun vs not. Derived: right count, all right.
- **energyScene:** story panels VillageArrival, SmokyChulha, PowerLine, DamTrip, CowShed, WindyRidge, SchoolRoof, HotSpring, RadioNews, Panchayat, SchoolLit.
