# Class 9 Science, Ch 8: Motion (plan)

Source: `source/iesc108.pdf` (NCERT Class 9 Science, 2020-21 edition; text extract beside it). Everything is kept: 8.1–8.6, Activities 8.1–8.11, Examples 8.1–8.7, every in-text question, the summary and Exercises 1–10. Built against `content/taste/`. Hindi with every string.

## Mission: Beat the school bus

The Class 10 books follow Riya (Class 10) and her brother Kabir; this is Kabir's own book, Class 9, the same family and the same year. His classmate Sania lives on the same road. Both take the school bus, which is always late, and Kabir claims he could beat it on his cycle. Sania says the bus is faster, it only *feels* slow. Grandpa, who once worked for the railways, refuses to judge: "Bring me numbers, not feelings." The science teacher turns it into the class project: **describe your journey to school so exactly that anyone could draw it.**

Every lesson gives Kabir one more tool, and every tool is tried on the same road:

1. Where things are, and how far they went: the bus stop as the reference point; the bus's route (distance) against the straight line home to school (displacement); the odometer (Activity 8.4, Example 8.2).
2. Steady or not: the cycle rolls at an even pace, the bus starts and stops (Table 8.1, Activity 8.5).
3. How fast: average speed from the odometer and the clock; speed with a direction is velocity; Usha's pool (Example 8.3) and Abdul's two-way trip (Exercise 3).
4. Speeding up and slowing down: the bus pulling away from a stop and braking (Example 8.4 is Rahul's bicycle; Exercises 2–3 of 8.3).
5. Drawing it: distance–time graphs of the cycle and the bus; Feroz and Sania's cycle ride (Activity 8.10, Table 8.5) is Sania's own data; the train timetable (Activity 8.9).
6. Drawing speed: velocity–time graphs from the bus's speedometer (Table 8.3); the area under the graph is the distance.
7. Predicting: the three equations of motion from the velocity–time graph; how far the bus travels before it stops when the driver brakes (Example 8.7), and why Grandpa keeps his distance from the car in front.
8. Round and round: the roundabout on the route; the athlete on the polygon track becoming a circle; a stone on a thread (Activity 8.11); the satellite (Exercise 10).

**Finale: race day.** Kabir and Sania leave together, Kabir on the cycle, Sania on the bus, each with a stopwatch and the odometer reading. That evening they draw both journeys on one distance–time graph for the notice board. The bus wins by two minutes, but the graph shows exactly where it lost time (four stops and the roundabout) and where it won (the straight stretch at 40 km/h). Grandpa signs the graph.

Family facts to keep: Kabir (Class 9), Riya (Class 10), Mom, Grandpa (ex-railways); the colony and the school bus route; Sania is a classmate, not family. The school bus, odometer and roundabout do not appear in the Class 10 books, so nothing collides.

## Lessons (in NCERT order)

1. **describing-motion** (intro, 8.1, 8.1.1; Activities 8.1–8.4; Examples: the 60 km and 35 km journey; in-text Q1–Q3)
   - Hook: the bus is late again; Kabir's claim; Grandpa wants numbers; the project. The roadmap.
   - Learn: rest and motion depend on who is looking (the train that seems to move, Activity 8.2; the trees running backwards); a **reference point** (the origin) before anything is "2 km away"; motion along a straight line (Fig 8.1); **distance** (the path, a number only) and **displacement** (start to finish, shortest, with a direction); displacement can be zero while distance is not; magnitude.
   - See it in life: the bus route is 6 km of road, but the school is 4 km from home as the crow flies; a round trip's displacement is zero.
   - Practice: Activity 8.3 (the basketball court) as a prop task, Activity 8.4 (odometer), in-text Q1–Q3 (the farmer on the square field).
   - Props: `motionTrack` (walk mode: drag the walker, read distance and displacement), `motionScene` LateBus.
2. **uniform-and-non-uniform** (8.1.2; Table 8.1, Activity 8.5)
   - Hook: Kabir's cycle ticks along; the bus lurches stop to stop.
   - Learn: **uniform motion** (equal distances in equal intervals; the interval must be small); **non-uniform motion**; reading Table 8.1 (object A uniform, B not).
   - See it in life: timing the cycle and the bus over four equal stretches of the road.
   - Practice: Table 8.1 as a sort-the-readings question; is-this-uniform from a list of readings.
   - Props: `motionTrack` (readings mode: an object leaves a tick every second; equal gaps or not).
3. **speed-and-velocity** (8.2, 8.2.1; Examples 8.1–8.3; Activities 8.6–8.7; in-text Q1–Q5)
   - Hook: "143 km/h" on the cricket telecast; the speed-limit board on the road (Fig 8.2).
   - Learn: **speed** = distance ÷ time; units m/s and km/h, and converting between them (× 1000 ÷ 3600); **average speed** = total distance ÷ total time; **velocity** = speed with a direction; **average velocity** = displacement ÷ time, and for uniform change the mean of u and v (Eq. 8.2); Usha swims 180 m and her average velocity is 0.
   - See it in life: the bus's odometer and clock give its average speed (Example 8.2's form); Kabir walks to the bus stop at 4 km/h (Activity 8.6); counting seconds after lightning (Activity 8.7).
   - Practice: Examples 8.1–8.3, in-text Q1–Q5 (the spaceship signal), Exercises 2 and 3 (Joseph's jog, Abdul's trip: average speed of a two-way trip is not the mean of the two speeds).
   - Props: `motionTrack` (speed mode: clock and odometer readouts; a one-way and a there-and-back journey), `motionScene` SpeedBoard.
4. **acceleration** (8.3; Example 8.4; Activity 8.8; in-text Q1–Q3)
   - Hook: the bus pulls away from the stop and everyone sways back; the brake throws everyone forward.
   - Learn: velocity changing with time; **acceleration** = change in velocity ÷ time (Eq. 8.3), m/s²; positive along the motion, negative against it (retardation); **uniform acceleration** (free fall) and non-uniform; Rahul's bicycle: 0 to 6 m/s in 30 s is 0.2 m/s², 6 to 4 m/s in 5 s is −0.4 m/s².
   - See it in life: the bus from 0 to 36 km/h in 10 s; the brake at the school gate.
   - Practice: Example 8.4, in-text Q1–Q3, Activity 8.8 (one example of each kind), Exercise 4 (the motorboat).
   - Props: `motionTrack` (acceleration mode: set u, v, t; watch the object and the velocity readout; the sign of a).
5. **distance-time-graphs** (8.4, 8.4.1; Fig 8.3–8.4; Table 8.2; Activities 8.9–8.10; in-text Q1–Q2)
   - Hook: Sania's notebook: distance from home every five minutes of her cycle ride (Table 8.5, Feroz and Sania). "Draw it," says Riya.
   - Learn: a line graph of distance against time; uniform motion is a straight line and its **slope** is the speed (the triangle ABC, Eq. 8.4); at rest is a horizontal line; accelerated motion curves upward (Table 8.2); reading speed off a graph; a train timetable as a graph (Activity 8.9).
   - See it in life: Kabir's and Sania's rides on one graph; the bus's graph has flat steps at every stop.
   - Practice: in-text Q1–Q2, Exercise 6 (three objects on one graph: who is fastest, do they meet), Exercise 9 (a) and (b).
   - Props: `motionGraph` (distance–time: an object moves on a road and its graph draws live; the slope triangle; plot a table of readings), `motionScene` Notebook.
6. **velocity-time-graphs** (8.4.2; Fig 8.5–8.7; Table 8.3; in-text Q3–Q4)
   - Hook: Grandpa rides along and reads the speedometer every five seconds.
   - Learn: velocity against time; uniform velocity is a horizontal line; the **area under the graph** is the distance (the rectangle, Fig 8.5); uniform acceleration is a sloping straight line (Table 8.3, Fig 8.6), and the area is a rectangle plus a triangle; a falling line is slowing down; any shape for non-uniform acceleration (Fig 8.7).
   - See it in life: the bus between two stops as a v–t graph: a rise, a flat top, a fall; the area under it must come to the distance between the stops.
   - Practice: in-text Q3–Q4, Exercise 5 (two cars braking: which travels further), Exercise 8 (the speed–time graph of a car: distance in the first 4 s), Exercise 9 (c).
   - Props: `motionGraph` (velocity–time: the same object, the graph draws live; shade the area and read the distance).
7. **equations-of-motion** (8.5–8.5.3; Fig 8.8; Examples 8.5–8.7; in-text Q1–Q5)
   - Hook: "How far does the bus go after the driver brakes?" Nobody wants to measure it with a stopwatch at the school gate.
   - Learn: from the velocity–time graph of uniform acceleration (Fig 8.8), one step per line: v = u + at (Eq. 8.5); s = ut + ½at² (the trapezium as rectangle plus triangle, Eq. 8.6); 2as = v² − u² (Eq. 8.7); which equation when (no t, no v, no s); units first, always m, s, m/s.
   - See it in life: Example 8.7 is the bus: braking at 6 m/s² for 2 s means 12 m of road, so keep your distance; the train (Example 8.5), the car (Example 8.6).
   - Practice: Examples 8.5–8.7, in-text Q1–Q5 (the stone thrown up: time and height), Exercises 7 (the ball dropped from 20 m) and 1 (the athlete's half round, which needs lesson 8 too, so it is set there).
   - Props: `motionGraph` (equations mode: the trapezium's parts labelled u, v, t, at; the three equations written out with the numbers put in).
8. **uniform-circular-motion** (8.6; Fig 8.9; Activity 8.11; Eq. 8.13)
   - Hook: the roundabout on the bus route; the speedometer steady but the bus turning.
   - Learn: velocity changes when direction changes, even at constant speed; the athlete on a square track turns four times, on a hexagon six, on an octagon eight… on a circle all the time; **uniform circular motion** is accelerated motion; speed round a circle, v = 2πr/t (Eq. 8.13); the stone on a thread leaves along the tangent when released (Activity 8.11); the moon, satellites, the hammer throw.
   - See it in life: the bus's speed round the roundabout from its radius and the time; the satellite (Exercise 10).
   - Practice: Exercise 1 (the athlete: distance and displacement after 2 min 20 s), Exercise 10, a tangent question (where does the stone go).
   - Finale: race day, both journeys on one graph, the eight stops recapped, Grandpa's signature.
   - Props: `circularTrack`, `motionScene` RaceDay.

## Props

The chapter is about one thing drawn two ways, an object on a road and the same motion as a graph, so it gets two mechanisms and one scene, not eight props. All are new to the library (`library/catalogue.md` checked: `plot.graph` only joins points and knows no time, `coordinatePlane` is geometry, `chanceRun` has a graph but of trials; none move an object along a line with a clock).

| Prop | What it shows | Learner can | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **motionTrack** (new, `@chalk/prop-motion`) | A straight road with a marked origin and landmarks (home, stop, school); one object (walker, cycle, bus) with its position, a clock, an odometer (distance) and a displacement arrow back to the start. Modes: *walk* (drag the object; distance and displacement update; a there-and-back shows displacement zero), *readings* (play; the object leaves a tick every second; equal or unequal gaps), *speed* (play a journey from a table of positions; average speed and average velocity worked out), *acceleration* (set u, v, t; play; velocity readout and a = (v − u)/t with its sign). | drag the object, play / pause / reset, set u, v, t, pick a journey | L1, L2, L3, L4 |
| **motionGraph** (new, same package) | The same object on a road at the top and its graph underneath, drawn live as it moves: distance–time or velocity–time, time on the x-axis. A slope triangle the learner can place between two points (reads speed); shading under a velocity–time graph (reads distance as rectangle + triangle); plotting a given table of readings point by point (Tables 8.1–8.5). *Equations* mode: the trapezium of Fig 8.8 with OA = u, BC = v, OC = t, BD = at labelled, and the three equations written out with the numbers. Journeys are given by the lesson as piecewise-constant acceleration segments, so every value is exact. | play / pause / scrub time, place the slope triangle, shade an interval, plot the next reading, change u, v, a, t | L5, L6, L7, finale |
| **circularTrack** (new, same package) | A runner on a closed track whose number of sides the learner changes (4, 6, 8, … → circle); the velocity arrow at the runner; a count of direction changes per lap; the circle's r and the lap time t with v = 2πr/t worked out; a stone-on-a-thread mode where releasing the thread sends the stone off along the tangent. | change the sides, play, release the stone | L8 |
| **motionScene** (new, `isExplainer: false`) | Story panels in the family's look: LateBus, ProjectNotice, OdometerDash, SpeedBoard, Notebook (Sania's table), SpeedometerRide, BrakeGate, Roundabout, RaceDay. | — | hooks and finale |

Shared library reuse: the question shapes (`choiceQuestion`, `goalQuestion`, `orderQuestion` from `@chalk/patterns`), `text`, `line`, `button` from basics. Numeric answers (an average speed, an acceleration, a distance from the equations) use `choice` with worked distractors (the mean-of-speeds slip, the forgotten unit conversion, the sign of a), so no new question prop is needed.

## Questions

One family per lesson at least, in the chapter's `questions/`: distance vs displacement (walks on the track), uniform-or-not (readings), average speed with unit conversion (km/h ↔ m/s), average speed of a two-way trip, acceleration with sign, speed from a distance–time slope, distance from a velocity–time area, which-equation-and-solve (one unknown), circular speed from r and t. Every row has a witness; distractors are the chapter's real slips.
