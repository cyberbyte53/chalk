## `decimal` — `@chalk/prop-decimal`

Turning a fraction p/q into a decimal by long division, one digit at a time. Each step multiplies the remainder by 10 and divides by q; the remainder is always less than q, so either a remainder becomes 0 (the expansion terminates) or a remainder comes back and the digits repeat from there (non-terminating recurring). It knows division, nothing about any lesson.

## `primes` — `@chalk/prop-primes`

Whole numbers and their primes: division with remainder, Euclid's algorithm, prime factorisation as a **bag of beads**, HCF and LCM, and what the bags say about square roots and decimals. Built for Class 10 Real Numbers, but each prop knows only its own world.

| Prop | Shows | Learner can | Used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sweetShare` | a sweets shared equally among b friends, one round at a time: each round every friend gets one sweet from the box. A round can be given only while the box holds at least b sweets, so the game ends by itself with fewer than b left. At the end the rounds are the quotient q and the box is the remainder r: a = b × q + r with 0 ≤ r < b, and only one such q and r. | `giveRound`, `startAgain`, `setSweets`, `setFriends` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
| `factorTree` | A factor tree. The number at the top is split into two factors, and each factor that isn't prime is split again, until every end is a prime. The primes at the ends are the number's prime factorisation; whichever splits are chosen, the ends are the same primes (the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic). | `select`, `split`, `setNumber`, `startAgain` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
| `stickMeasure` | Two sticks, a and b. Lay the shorter along the longer as many times as it fits (a = b × q + r) and keep the leftover r; then do the same with b and r, until nothing is left over. The last stick laid is the longest piece that measures both a and b exactly: HCF(a, b). Marks of a chosen piece length show that a piece measuring both sticks measures the leftover too. | `lay`, `keep`, `startAgain`, `setSticks`, `tryPiece` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
| `lapRace` | Two runners on a round track start together from the start line; one takes a minutes a round, the other b. Play runs the clock for both at once; each minute a runner is back at the start is written in their log (Sonia: 18, 36; Ravi: 12, 24, 36). They are at the start together at a minute in both logs, a common multiple of a and b, first at LCM(a, b). Passing each other elsewhere on the track doesn't count. | `play`, `pause`, `startAgain` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
| `primeTowers` | Two numbers' prime factorisations as towers: for each prime, the two numbers' towers side by side, each as tall as that prime's power in the number (18 = 2 × 3²: a 2-tower of 1 and a 3-tower of 2). HCF: a line at the shorter tower's top in each column (each prime at its smaller power). LCM: a line at the taller tower's top (each prime at its greater power). HCF × LCM: both lines; shorter + taller = the two heights together, so HCF × LCM = a × b. | `show`, `setNumbers` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
| `tenMaker` | A fraction p/q as prime beads, the numerator's above the bar and the denominator's below. Cancel crosses out the primes they share (lowest terms). Pair links each 2 in the denominator with a 5: a ten. Add puts in the missing 2s or 5s, top and bottom alike, so every bead below is in a ten and the denominator is 10ⁿ (3/8 = 375/1000 = 0.375). A 3, 7 or any other prime below has no partner, so the decimal never terminates. | `setFraction`, `cancel`, `pair`, `topUp`, `startAgain` | class10-maths/real-numbers |

The shares that end are the n whose only primes are 2 and 5. | `split` | class10-maths/real-numbers |
