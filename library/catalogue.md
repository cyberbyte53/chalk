Each prop: its address, its role, what it is for and its world (its `describe`), its fields, its actions and its signals, and the chapters that place it. ## `decimal` — `@chalk/prop-decimal`

Turning a fraction p/q into a decimal by long division, one digit at a time. Each step multiplies the remainder by 10 and divides by q; the remainder is always less than q, so either a remainder becomes 0 (the expansion terminates) or a remainder comes back and the digits repeat from there (non-terminating recurring). It knows division, nothing about any lesson.

### `sweetShare` · explainer

`library/props/primes/sweetShare` · used in class10-maths/real-numbers

a sweets shared equally among b friends, one round at a time: each round every friend gets one sweet from the box. A round can be given only while the box holds at least b sweets, so the game ends by itself with fewer than b left. At the end the rounds are the quotient q and the box is the remainder r: a = b × q + r with 0 ≤ r < b, and only one such q and r.

### `primeTowers` · explainer

`library/props/primes/primeTowers` · used in class10-maths/real-numbers

Two numbers' prime factorisations as towers: for each prime, the two numbers' towers side by side, each as tall as that prime's power in the number (18 = 2 × 3²: a 2-tower of 1 and a 3-tower of 2). HCF: a line at the shorter tower's top in each column (each prime at its smaller power). LCM: a line at the taller tower's top (each prime at its greater power). HCF × LCM: both lines; shorter + taller = the two heights together, so HCF × LCM = a × b.

### `tenMaker` · explainer

`library/props/primes/tenMaker` · used in class10-maths/real-numbers

A fraction p/q as prime beads, the numerator's above the bar and the denominator's below. Cancel crosses out the primes they share (lowest terms). Pair links each 2 in the denominator with a 5: a ten. Add puts in the missing 2s or 5s, top and bottom alike, so every bead below is in a ten and the denominator is 10ⁿ (3/8 = 375/1000 = 0.375). A 3, 7 or any other prime below has no partner, so the decimal never terminates.
